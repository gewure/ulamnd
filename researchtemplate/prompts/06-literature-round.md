# Literature round

*Run this at initialization, before any novelty claim, and whenever the agent's answers become vague —
vagueness is the signal that the ground is not solid.*

---

Literature round on <topic / the specific object or claim>.

1. What is known about this, by whom, and where. Prefer the original source to a survey, and the survey to
   your memory of the field.
2. For each source: what does it actually state — hypotheses included — in one or two lines? Not what it
   is about; what it says.
3. Which of our statements does it already contain, imply, refute, or make routine? Be specific about
   which of ours, and quote the relevant statement.
4. What is the standard name for the object we have been calling <our name>? If the field has a name and a
   normalisation for it, adopt theirs and record the translation.
5. What have we not looked at that a referee in this field would immediately ask about?

Write the result into `research/LITERATURE.md`, one entry per source, each marked **read** (you opened the
source), **abstract only**, or **unverified — from memory**. Anything in the third category may not be
cited in a document until it has moved to the first. If a source is behind a paywall or you cannot access
it, say so rather than reconstructing its contents.

Then: list every novelty claim we currently make, and against each, the specific source that was checked.
Any claim without one is downgraded today.
