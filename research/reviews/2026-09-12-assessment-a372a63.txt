   Verdict: a substantive exploratory mathematics project, but not a reliable collection of proved results in
   its current form. The present manuscripts contain a false unsmoothed claim, an incorrect general explicit
   formula, and a significant defect in a spectral proof.

This assessment concerns the revision linked in reference [1]. It is not an allegation of fraud, a judgment about the
author’s motives, or a rejection of every result in the repository. It is a targeted assessment of whether the current
mathematical claims can be relied on.

What is credible
The underlying mathematics is genuine: polynomial prime values, Bateman–Horn constants, pair singular series,
Dedekind zeta functions, and modular spectral theory are established subjects. Goldston and Suriajaya’s cited
work really does connect singular-series averages to zeta zeros. [9]
There is also a checkable algebraic core. Independently written arithmetic checks verified the finite-prime version
of paper I’s main exact identity in 24 cases across four polynomials, with exact rational equality. This supports
that algebraic identity; it does not establish the later infinite-product or asymptotic conclusions.
The repository discloses extensive AI assistance, records corrections, and rejects its original Fibonacci-dimension
and quasicrystal suggestions rather than continuing to promote them. Those are positive signs of transparency.
[7] [8]

What prevents endorsement
   • Paper I: the claimed bounded error for the unsmoothed sum is impossible even for the classical linear
     polynomial. A short contradiction is given on page 2.
   • Paper II: the general explicit formula assumes a pole is simple when it can be double. A cyclic cubic example
     requires an additional logarithm; see page 3.
   • Paper IV: the proof replaces a restricted arithmetic sum with a complete modular-orbit sum, dropping a
     condition that matters; see page 4.
The main nonlinear smoothed conjecture remains open, and statements about actual prime counts require Hardy–
Littlewood-type assumptions. The project is not a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis or of Bateman–Horn. Its own
notes report that professional feedback is still being sought. [2] [6] [7]

Consequence for the theorem

  Established: the complete-orbit identity does not describe the restricted arithmetic sum as written. The

   • Finding 2: the rational-root and discriminant calculations, character values, and trivial-character multiplicity
     were recomputed independently.
   • Finding 3: the modular transformation and coprimality conditions were checked directly; both associated
     quadratic forms have discriminant −4.
These checks were written independently rather than obtained by running the repository’s own test suite. Agree­
ment in finite arithmetic does not certify analytic continuation, error estimates, or exchanges of infinite sums.

Limits and recommendation
This is a targeted review, not a full referee report or a complete literature/novelty audit. The full numerical studies
were not reproduced, and software execution or supply-chain security was not audited. No conclusion about fraud,
motives, or the value of every unpublished idea follows from these findings.
Recommendation: treat the repository as exploratory research with potentially useful identities and numerical
observations. Do not cite its new theorems as established or endorse the present manuscripts without substantial
corrections and specialist review. AI involvement is not the basis for this judgment; the mathematical counterex­
ample and proof defects are.
