\author{Jasper Reichardt \and Claude Fable 5.1}

\begin{abstract}
For an irreducible quadratic $f$ with pair singular series $S_f(h)$ and Bateman--Horn constant $C(f)$,
parts~I and~II reduced the conjecture $\sum_{h\le H}(1-h/H)(S_f(h)-C(f)^2)=-\tfrac12C(f)\log H+A_f+o(1)$ to a statement
about the pairs of distinct roots of $f$ modulo squarefree $d$ (``Hypothesis~(E)''), and showed that in
Ces\`aro form any unbounded saving over the trivial bound suffices. This paper takes Hypothesis~(E) apart. We
prove an exact decomposition of the Ces\`aro off-diagonal into pieces indexed by the part $u$ of the modulus on
which the two roots agree: piece $u$ is the same problem with length $H/u$ and with the roots of
$x^2\equiv D$ replaced by the roots of $u^2x^2\equiv D$, a dilation by $u^{-1}$, and all pieces carry equal
weight per unit of $\log u$. The main result is that the off-diagonal is not small: the pieces with $u>H^{2/3}$,
where the dilations average out along $u$, leave a main term $c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H$ with an explicit negative
constant, $-\log p/(p-1)$ per split prime modulus, and this constant predicts the mean of the sharp off-diagonal
to three digits against exact data to $10^7$ for four quadratics. Two further parts are unconditional: the piece
$u=1$, by the theorem of Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec on Weyl sums for quadratic roots converted from smooth to
sharp cut-offs; and the moduli below the length in every piece, by Koksma's inequality and a weighted form of the
Chinese-remainder equidistribution of Kowalski and Soundararajan, whose local input is the same for every
dilation. What remains is
one window in each piece with $1<u\le H^{2/3}$, the moduli between the length and $u^2$ times the length, where
an interval covers less than a period and a logarithmic saving is not enough. We give its exact Fourier form,
show that a Weyl-sum hypothesis (Hypothesis~W) closes it only if its exponents satisfy $5\theta+6B<3$, which
demands square-root cancellation with almost no loss in the dilation, and prove the Ces\`aro form of
Conjecture~1, constant included, under that hypothesis. Over $\F_q[u]$ we prove that the off-diagonal has a main term:
for $f=t^2-u$ and the two smallest lengths, $\Off_f(N)=-2/q+O(q^{-3/2})$; a configuration count predicts, and
exact computations for three discriminants and $q\le43$ confirm, that the generic value is $-1/q$, the weight of
the excluded diagonal pairs at the first degree above the cut-off, the value $-2/q$ being a small-length effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{In plain terms.} Prime values of a polynomial are correlated through the small primes. Part~I
turned the size of that correlation into an exact identity whose main part, coming from equal roots of $f$
modulo $d$, is understood, and whose remaining part, coming from pairs of distinct roots, is a question about
how the square roots of the discriminant modulo composite numbers are distributed. This paper shows that
the remaining part is a sum of copies of one problem, one copy for each way the two roots can agree on a part
of the modulus; that the copies are of equal importance; that the first copy is a theorem of Duke, Friedlander
and Iwaniec; that in every copy the moduli shorter than the length are handled by a general mixing property
of the Chinese remainder theorem; that the copies with a large agreeing part average out to an explicit main
term, which we compute and confirm numerically; and that what is left is, in each copy, the moduli just above
the length, where an interval covers less than one period, and where a genuinely stronger estimate is needed.
We state that estimate precisely and say what strength it must have. Over polynomial rings the same object has an
explicit main term too, which we compute.

\paragraph{Set-up.} Let $f=t^2+bt+c\in\Z[t]$ be irreducible, without fixed prime divisor, with discriminant
$D=b^2-4c$ (not a square). For a prime $p$ let $\omega(p)=\omega_f(p)$ be the number of roots of $f$ modulo $p$:
$\omega(p)=2$ if $p\nmid2D$ and $D$ is a square modulo $p$ (\emph{split}), $0$ if $D$ is a non-square (\emph{inert}),
and $\le1$ if $p\mid2D$ (\emph{special}). All moduli below are squarefree. Put
\[
\lambda(p)=\frac p{p-2\omega(p)},\qquad P_p=1-\frac{\omega(p)^2}{(p-\omega(p))^2},\qquad P(1)=\prod_pP_p,\qquad
W_f(d)=\frac{P(1)\lambda(d)}d,\qquad w(u)=u\,\omega(u)\,W_f(u)=P(1)\lambda(u)\omega(u),
\]
$\lambda$ multiplicative; this is the weight of part~I, $W_f(d)=d\,b(d)\prod_{p\nmid d}P_p$ with $b(p)=(p-\omega(p))^{-2}$,
since $p\,b(p)/P_p=1/(p-2\omega(p))$. If $\omega(2)=1$ (as for $t^2+1$) then $P_2=0$ and $\lambda(2)$ is undefined; in
that case every $d$ in the support of $W_f$ is even, $W_f(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}p(p-\omega(p))^{-2}\prod_{p\nmid d}P_p$, and all
statements below hold verbatim with the weight $w(u)=u\omega(u)W_f(u)$ (which is then supported on even $u$), the only
property used being $W_f(ud')=W_f(u)\lambda(d')/d'$ for $d'$ split and coprime to $u$. In every case $w=c_f\,g$ with
$c_f>0$ and $g$ multiplicative on squarefree integers, $g(p)=\lambda(p)\omega(p)$ for odd $p$, $g(2)\in\{0,1\}$; we write
$\lambda\omega$ for $g$ when the distinction is immaterial. One sign deserves notice: $\lambda(3)=-3$ and $P_3=-3$ when
$\omega(3)=2$ (as for $t^2+2$ or $2t^2+1$), so the weights are not always positive. All upper bounds below are
bounds for absolute values and use $|\lambda|$, $|w|$; Proposition~\ref{prop:KSw} is applied to $|w|$; and the mean
value $m$ of $g$ in~\eqref{eq:md} is nonzero but may be negative. Nothing else depends on the sign. For $d$ coprime to $2D$ let $R_d=\{r\bmod d:\ r^2\equiv D\}$ (so $|R_d|=\omega(d)$),
the roots of $f$ being $s=(r-b)/2$. Write $\rep xd\in[1,d]$ for the representative of $x\bmod d$, and, for a length
$Y>0$ and a residue $m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B}
B^{(Y)}_d(m)=\sum_{\substack{h\le Y\\ h\equiv m\ (d)}}(Y-h)-\frac{Y^2}{2d}.
\end{equation}
Part~I, Theorem~6, proved that with $C=C(f)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cesaro}
\sum_{h\le H}\Bigl(1-\frac hH\Bigr)\bigl(S_f(h)-C^2\bigr)=-\tfrac12C\log H+O_f(1)+\frac{C^2}H\Off^\ast_f(H),\qquad
\Off^\ast_f(H)=\sum_{d}W_f(d)\sum_{\substack{s,s'\ \mathrm{roots\ of\ }f\bmod d\\ s\ne s'}}B^{(H)}_d(s'-s),
\end{equation}
the series converging absolutely, and that $\Off^\ast_f(H)\ll H\log H$ trivially. \emph{Hypothesis~(E) in
Ces\`aro form} is $\Off^\ast_f(H)=o(H\log H)$; it implies Conjecture~1 of part~I in Ces\`aro form with the leading
term $-\tfrac12C(f)\log H$ (part~I, Theorem~6(ii) and Remark~7, since the Galois group $S_2$ is $2$-transitive).

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Decomposition} (Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces}): $\Off^\ast_f(H)=P(1)\sum_u\lambda(u)\omega(u)\,\mathcal P_u(H/u)$,
the sum over squarefree $u$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$, where the piece $\mathcal P_u(Y)$ is the sum~\eqref{eq:cesaro} with
$H$ replaced by $Y$ and the roots $R_{d'}$ replaced by the roots $R^{(u)}_{d'}$ of $u^2x^2\equiv D$, over moduli
$d'$ coprime to $u$ all of whose primes are split. Each piece is $\ll Y$, and $\sum_u\lambda(u)\omega(u)H/u\asymp H\log H$.
\item \emph{The first piece} (Theorem~\ref{thm:u1}): $\mathcal P_1(Y)\ll Y^{1-\delta}$ unconditionally when $D$
is a fundamental discriminant covered by \cite{DFI2012}; the input is a sharp-cut-off form of their Weyl-sum
bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp}).
\item \emph{Far moduli} (Lemma~\ref{lem:far}): in piece $u$ the moduli $d'>u^2YL$ contribute $O(Y/(u^2L))$, by a
trivial count; no reflection argument is needed.
\item \emph{Large agreeing part} (Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII}): $\sum_{u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\mathcal P_u(H/u)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H+O(H^{1-\delta})$,
by averaging the dilations along $u$ (the input is the distribution of $\lambda\omega$ in arithmetic progressions),
with the explicit constant $c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)=-\sum_{p\ \mathrm{split}}\lambda(p)M_p\log p/(p(p-1))<0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}: the dilation average of the Ces\`aro weight is $-\log p/(p-1)$ for a prime modulus and $0$
for a composite one). For $t^2+1$, $c_{\mathrm{off}}=-0.13439$; the mean of the sharp off-diagonal $\Off_f(t)$ to $10^7$ is
$-0.0098\pm0.0007$ against the predicted $c_{\mathrm{off}}+(1-D_f(1))/2=-0.00978$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:coff}).
\item \emph{Small moduli of every piece} (Theorem~\ref{thm:small}): the part of $\mathcal P_u(Y)$ from
moduli $d'\le Y$ is $\ll Y(\log Y)^{-c}$ uniformly in $u$, by Koksma's inequality and a weighted version of
the Chinese-remainder equidistribution theorem of Kowalski and Soundararajan~\cite{KowalskiSoundararajan2021}
(Proposition~\ref{prop:KSw}). Hence the small-moduli part of $\Off^\ast_f$ is $o(H\log H)$ unconditionally.
\item \emph{The window} (\S\ref{sec:W}): unconditionally (Theorem~\ref{thm:Aprime})
$\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}H+\sum_{u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\mathcal W_u(H/u;\log H)+O(H(\log H)^{1-c})$, where the
window $\mathcal W_u$ collects the moduli $d'\in(Y,u^2Y\log H]$ of piece $u$, on which the interval $[1,Y]$ is shorter
than the period. The window is the core of Hypothesis~(E): a logarithmic saving there is not enough. We give its
exact finite Fourier expansion (Lemma~\ref{lem:finfourier}), which has $u^2\log H$ frequencies of full weight per
modulus, and prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:A}) that Hypothesis~W$(\theta,B)$, a Weyl-sum bound $\ll(uk)^Bt^{\theta}$ for the
dilated roots, gives $\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}H+O(H^{1-\delta})$, hence the Ces\`aro form of Conjecture~1 with
$A_f=A^{\mathrm{diag}}_f+C(f)^2c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)$, provided $5\theta+6B<3$ and $2B(2-\theta)<1-\theta$. These conditions
exclude the exponents of \cite{DFI2012} and demand square-root cancellation with a loss below $(uk)^{1/12}$
(Remark~\ref{rem:exponents}).
\item \emph{Function field} (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}): for $f=t^2-u$ over $\F_q[u]$ and $N\in\{1,2\}$,
$\Off_f(N)=-2/q+O(q^{-3/2})$; Conjecture~\ref{conj:kappa}: $\Off_f(N)=-1/q+O(q^{-3/2})$ whenever
$\max(2N-2,\deg D)\ge N+1$, i.e.\ for all $N\ge3$ and for $N\le2$ when $\deg D>N$, and $-2/q$ otherwise.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is new relative to Hooley.} The decomposition into pieces is implicit in Hooley's treatment of
$\sum_{n\le x}\tau(n^2+a)$~\cite{Hooley1963}, whose sum $\Sigma_5$ is our $\Off$ with the weight $1/k$ and
whose Lemma~5 handles the phase that our piece $u=1$ carries; what is new is the identification of the pieces
$u>1$ as dilated copies, their equal weight, the averaging argument for large $u$ and the main term
$c_{\mathrm{off}}H$ it produces, the unconditional treatment of the small moduli of every piece, the precise statement
of what remains with the exponents it requires, and the function-field main term.

\section{The exact decomposition}\label{sec:pieces}

For squarefree $d$ coprime to $2D$, the roots of $f$ modulo $d$ are $s=(r-b)/2$, $r\in R_d$, and $R_d=\prod_{p\mid d}R_p$
by the Chinese remainder theorem, each $R_p$ being $\{\pm r_p\}$. Two roots $s\ne s'$ therefore agree exactly on a
divisor $u\mid d$ and differ exactly on $d'=d/u>1$, where every prime of $d'$ is split; and on $d'$ we have
$r'\equiv-r$, so $s'-s\equiv-r\pmod{d'}$ and $s'-s\equiv0\pmod u$. Let $\bar u$ denote the inverse of $u$
modulo $d'$. Then $s'-s\equiv u\bar u\cdot(-r)\pmod d$, i.e.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m}
\rep{s'-s}d=u\,\rep{-\bar ur}{d'} .
\end{equation}
If $d$ has a special prime $p$ ($\omega(p)=1$) the two roots agree at $p$, so $p\mid u$; inert primes do not divide $d$
at all ($\omega(d)=0$). Define, for squarefree $u$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$ and squarefree $d'$ coprime to $u$ with all primes
split,
\[
R^{(u)}_{d'}=\{-\bar ur\bmod d':\ r\in R_{d'}\}=\{x\bmod d':\ u^2x^2\equiv D\ (d')\},
\]
the roots of the fixed quadratic $Q_u(x)=u^2x^2-D$ modulo $d'$ (the equality because $x\mapsto-\bar ux$ is a bijection
$R_{d'}\to R^{(u)}_{d'}$ when $(u,d')=1$), and the \emph{piece}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:piece}
\mathcal P_u(Y)=\sum_{d'}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}\bigl(\rep x{d'}\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[pieces]\label{prop:pieces}
For every $H\ge1$,
\[
\Off^\ast_f(H)=\sum_{u}w(u)\,\mathcal P_u(H/u),\qquad w(u)=u\,\omega(u)\,W_f(u),
\]
the sum over squarefree $u\ge1$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$, every series converging absolutely. Moreover
$\mathcal P_u(Y)\ll_fY$ uniformly in $u$, $\sum_{u\le t}|w(u)|\ll t$, and $\sum_{u\le H}|w(u)|/u\asymp\log H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $d=ud'$ as above. For $m=u\mu$ with $\mu\in[1,d']$ we have, writing $h=uh'$,
\[
B^{(H)}_{ud'}(u\mu)=\sum_{\substack{h'\le H/u\\ h'\equiv\mu\ (d')}}(H-uh')-\frac{H^2}{2ud'}=u\,B^{(H/u)}_{d'}(\mu).
\]
The ordered pairs $(s,s')$ of distinct roots modulo $d$ with agreeing part $u$ are in bijection with pairs
$(s,d')$, and by~\eqref{eq:m} their difference is $u\rep{-\bar ur}{d'}$ where $r$ is the $d'$-component of $2s+b$;
as $s$ runs over the $\omega(u)\omega(d')$ roots, $r$ runs $\omega(u)$ times over $R_{d'}$. Hence
\[
\sum_{s\ne s'}B^{(H)}_d(s'-s)=\sum_{\substack{d'\mid d,\ d'>1\\ d'\ \mathrm{split}}}\omega(u)\,u\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}B^{(H/u)}_{d'}\bigl(\rep{-\bar ur}{d'}\bigr),
\]
and with $W_f(ud')=P(1)\lambda(u)\lambda(d')/(ud')$ the claimed formula follows by summing over $d$; absolute
convergence is inherited from~\eqref{eq:cesaro}, all terms being regrouped without change of absolute value.
For the bound on $\mathcal P_u(Y)$: if $d'\le Y$ then, by the formula
$B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=-Ym/d'+m^2/(2d')+Y/2-m/2+(d'/2)\phi(\{(Y-m)/d'\})$ of part~I (proof of Theorem~6; $\phi(\theta)=\theta-\theta^2$),
valid for $1\le m\le d'\le Y$, the terms linear in $m$ cancel between $m$ and $d'-m$, and
$B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)+B^{(Y)}_{d'}(d'-m)=-m(d'-m)/d'+\tfrac{d'}2\bigl(\phi(\{\tfrac{Y-m}{d'}\})+\phi(\{\tfrac{Y+m}{d'}\})\bigr)\in[-d'/4,d'/4]$;
since $R^{(u)}_{d'}$ is symmetric, $|\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})|\le\omega(d')d'/8$ (individual values
$B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)$ can be as large as $Y/2$; the symmetry of the root set is used throughout). This gives
$\sum_{d'\le Y}\lambda(d')\omega(d')\ll Y$ by Shiu's theorem, since $\lambda\omega$ is multiplicative with $\lambda\omega(p)\le2p/(p-4)$
on split primes and $0$ on inert ones, of mean value $\asymp1$. If $d'>Y$ then $B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=(Y-m)^+-Y^2/(2d')$
for $m\in[1,d']$; the second term contributes $\ll Y^2\sum_{d'>Y}\lambda(d')\omega(d')/d'^2\ll Y$, and the first
contributes $\sum_{x\le Y}(Y-x)\sum_{d'>Y,\ d'\mid Q_u(x)}\lambda(d')/d'$; since $\sum_{d'>Y,\ d'\mid n}1/d'\le n^{-1}\sum_{e\mid n,\ e<n/Y}e$,
and on average over $x\le Y$, $\sum_{e<n/Y,\ e\mid Q_u(x)}e\le\sum_{e<u^2Y}e\,\rho_u(e)\,\mathbf 1_{e\mid Q_u(x)}$ has
mean $\ll\sum_{e<u^2Y}\rho_u(e)\ll u^2Y$ ($\rho_u(e)\le\omega(e)$ the number of roots of $Q_u$ modulo $e$, of mean value
$\ll1$), while $Q_u(x)\gg u^2x^2$, the first term is $\ll\sum_{x\le Y}(Y-x)\,Y/x^2\cdot\mathbf 1_{Q_u(x)>Y}+\sum_{x\le\sqrt Y/u}Y\ll Y$.
Finally $\sum_{u\le t}|w(u)|\ll t$ and $\sum_{u\le H}|w(u)|/u\asymp\log H$ because $|g|$ has a positive mean value on squarefree
integers (its Dirichlet series is $\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_D)\times$ an absolutely convergent product near $s=1$).
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} The off-diagonal is a sum over $u$ of the same object, evaluated at length $H/u$, for the
roots of $u^2x^2\equiv D$ instead of $x^2\equiv D$; since $\sum_uw(u)/u$ grows like $\log H$ while each piece
is $O(H/u)$, the pieces are equally important on a logarithmic scale, and no range of $u$ can be discarded.
The dilation by $\bar u$ is the whole difficulty: for $u=1$ the roots are those of a fixed fundamental
quadratic; for $u>1$ they are those of a quadratic whose discriminant $4u^2D$ grows with $u$, and existing
theorems degrade with the discriminant.

\section{Reduction of a piece to Weyl sums}\label{sec:reduction}

Fix $u$ and write $Y$ for the length, $R'_{d'}=R^{(u)}_{d'}$, $\rho(d')=|R'_{d'}|$. Split $\mathcal P_u(Y)=\mathcal P^{\le}_u(Y)+\mathcal P^{>}_u(Y)$
according to $d'\le Y$ or $d'>Y$.

\subsection{Small moduli: Fourier expansion and partial summation}

For $d'\le Y$ and $m\in[1,d']$, with $\phi(\theta)=\theta-\theta^2$ (part~I, proof of Theorem~6),
\[
B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=-\frac{Ym}{d'}+\frac{m^2}{2d'}+\frac Y2-\frac m2+\frac{d'}2\phi\Bigl(\Bigl\{\frac{Y-m}{d'}\Bigr\}\Bigr),
\]
and summing over the symmetric set $R'_{d'}$ ($x\in R'\Rightarrow-x\in R'$, so $m$ and $d'-m$ occur together) gives
$\sum_{x\in R'}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})=\sum_{\{x,-x\}}\bigl[-\tfrac{m(d'-m)}{d'}+\tfrac{d'}2\bigl(\phi(\{\tfrac{Y-m}{d'}\})+\phi(\{\tfrac{Y+m}{d'}\})\bigr)\bigr]$.
Since $\phi(\{\theta\})=\tfrac16-\pi^{-2}\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}\cos2\pi k\theta$ and $\tfrac16-\theta(1-\theta)=\pi^{-2}\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-2}\cos2\pi k\theta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier}
\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})=\frac{d'}{2\pi^2}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{k^2}\bigl(1-\cos\tfrac{2\pi kY}{d'}\bigr)\,\rho_k(d'),\qquad
\rho_k(d')=\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}\e\Bigl(\frac{kx}{d'}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
an absolutely convergent expansion for each $d'$ ($\rho_k(d')$ is real by symmetry). Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ple}
\mathcal P^\le_u(Y)=\frac1{2\pi^2}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{k^2}\,T_k(Y),\qquad T_k(Y)=\sum_{d'\le Y}\lambda(d')\rho_k(d')\bigl(1-\cos\tfrac{2\pi kY}{d'}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where now the interchange is legitimate because $d'$ ranges over a finite set, and trivially $|T_k(Y)|\le2\sum_{d'\le Y}\lambda\rho\ll Y$.

\begin{lemma}[from sharp Weyl sums to twisted sums]\label{lem:partial}
Suppose that for some $\theta<1$, $B\ge0$ and all $k\ge1$, $t\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
S_k(t):=\sum_{d'\le t}\lambda(d')\rho_k(d')\ll(uk)^{B}\,t^{\theta}.
\end{equation}
Then for every $Y_0\in[1,Y]$,
\[
T_k(Y)\ll Y_0\log Y_0+(uk)^B\bigl(Y^\theta+kY\,Y_0^{\theta-1}\bigr)\log Y .
\]
In particular, with $Y_0=(kY)^{1/(2-\theta)}$, $T_k(Y)\ll(uk)^B(kY)^{1/(2-\theta)}\log Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The moduli $d'\le Y_0$ contribute at most $2\sum_{d'\le Y_0}\lambda\rho\ll Y_0\log Y_0$ (crudely). On a dyadic block
$V<d'\le2V$, $V\ge Y_0$, partial summation against $S_k$ with the function $g(t)=1-\cos(2\pi kY/t)$, which satisfies
$|g|\le2$ and $|g'(t)|\le2\pi kY/t^2$, gives
$\bigl|\sum_{V<d'\le2V}\lambda\rho_k\,g\bigr|\le|S_k(2V)g(2V)|+|S_k(V)g(V)|+\int_V^{2V}|S_k(t)g'(t)|\,dt\ll(uk)^BV^\theta(1+kY/V)$.
Summing over the dyadic $V\in[Y_0,Y]$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} The phase $\cos(2\pi kY/d')$ oscillates in $d'$ once $d'\ll kY$, and the cost of integrating
it against a partial sum with a power saving is a factor $kY/d'$; the small moduli, where that factor is large,
are handled trivially, and the balance point $Y_0=(kY)^{1/(2-\theta)}$ leaves a power saving. Nothing more than a
sharp-cut-off Weyl bound with polynomial dependence on the frequency is used.

\subsection{Small moduli without the phase: Koksma and the mixing of the Chinese remainder theorem}\label{sec:koksma}

The Fourier route makes the phase $\cos(2\pi kY/d')$ appear; the discrepancy route does not. For $d'\le Y$ and
the symmetric set $R'_{d'}$, the identity before~\eqref{eq:fourier} reads
\[
\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})=\frac{d'}2\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}\Psi_{Y/d'}\Bigl(\Bigl\{\frac x{d'}\Bigr\}\Bigr),\qquad
\Psi_\eta(\theta)=-\theta(1-\theta)+\tfrac12\bigl(\phi(\{\eta-\theta\})+\phi(\{\eta+\theta\})\bigr),
\]
and $\Psi_\eta$ has mean $0$ on $[0,1)$ and total variation $\le3$, uniformly in $\eta$. Koksma's inequality
therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koksma}
\Bigl|\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})\Bigr|\le\tfrac32\,d'\,\omega(d')\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta^{(u)}_{d'}),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta^{(u)}_{d'}$ is the probability measure $\omega(d')^{-1}\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}\delta_{\{x/d'\}}$ and $\mathrm{disc}$ its
discrepancy, $\sup_I|\Delta(I)-|I||$ over intervals $I\subset\R/\Z$. The point is that~\eqref{eq:koksma} does not
see the length $Y$ at all.

We shall need a weighted form of Rankin's bound for smooth numbers, with Dickman-type decay.

\begin{lemma}[weighted smooth numbers]\label{lem:rankin}
Let $w\ge0$ be multiplicative, supported on squarefree integers, with $w(p)\le C_0$ for all primes $p$, where
$C_0\ge1$. There are an absolute constant $z_0$ and $u_0=16eC_0^2$ such that for $z\ge z_0$, $X\ge1$ and
$u:=\log X/\log z\in[u_0,\log z]$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{s>X\\ P^+(s)\le z}}\frac{w(s)}{s}\ \le\ \exp\Bigl(-\frac{u\log u}2\Bigr)\prod_{p\le z}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}p\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\delta>0$ (Rankin), the sum is at most
$X^{-\delta}\sum_{P^+(s)\le z}w(s)s^{\delta-1}=X^{-\delta}\prod_{p\le z}(1+w(p)p^{\delta-1})$, and
$1+ap^\delta\le(1+a)\exp(a(p^\delta-1))$ for $a\ge0$ gives
\[
\prod_{p\le z}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}{p^{1-\delta}}\Bigr)\le\prod_{p\le z}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}p\Bigr)\exp\bigl(C_0E\bigr),\qquad
E=\sum_{p\le z}\frac{p^\delta-1}{p}.
\]
Put $\lambda=\delta\log z$ and $g(v)=(e^{\delta v}-1)/v$, an increasing function of $v>0$, so that $(p^\delta-1)/p=g(\log p)\log p/p$.
With $S(t)=\sum_{p\le t}\log p/p=\log t+R(t)$, $|R(t)|\le R_0$ for $t\ge2$ (Mertens), Stieltjes integration gives
\[
E=\int_{2^-}^{z}g(\log t)\,dS(t)=\int_{\log2}^{\log z}g(v)\,dv+\int_{2^-}^{z}g(\log t)\,dR(t)
\le\int_0^{\lambda}\frac{e^{s}-1}{s}\,ds+3R_0\,g(\log z)+g(\log2)\log2,
\]
where we used that $g$ is increasing to bound $\int R\,dg$. Since $(e^s-1)/s\le e^s$, the integral is $\le e^\lambda$; the
second term is $\le3R_0e^\lambda/\log z$; and $g(\log2)\log2=2^\delta-1\le1$ as $\delta\le1$ (because $\lambda\le\log u\le\log\log z$).
Hence $E\le2e^\lambda$ once $\log z\ge6R_0$ and $e^\lambda\ge2$. Now choose $\lambda=\log u-\log(4C_0)$, which is $\ge\log4$ for
$u\ge16C_0$ and is $\le\log u\le\log z$ as required: then $e^\lambda=u/(4C_0)$, so $C_0E\le u/2$, while
$\delta\log X=\lambda u=u\log u-u\log(4C_0)$. The exponent is therefore at most
$-u\log u+u\log(4C_0)+u/2\le-\tfrac12u\log u$ as soon as $\tfrac12\log u\ge\log(4C_0)+\tfrac12$, i.e.\ $u\ge16eC_0^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[weighted Kowalski--Soundararajan]\label{prop:KSw}
Let $(A_p)_p$ be a family of subsets $A_p\subset\Z/p$, $\rho(p)=|A_p|$, let $Q$ be the set of squarefree $q$ all
of whose prime factors have $\rho(p)\ge1$, and for $q\in Q$ let $A_q\subset\Z/q$ be the set of classes reducing into
$A_p$ for every $p\mid q$, $\Delta_q=\rho(q)^{-1}\sum_{a\in A_q}\delta_{\{a/q\}}$, $\rho(q)=\prod_{p\mid q}\rho(p)$. Let $w\ge0$ be a
multiplicative function supported on squarefree integers with $w(p)\le C_0$ ($C_0\ge1$) and $w(p)=0$ unless
$\rho(p)\ge1$, and suppose that for some $\beta,\gamma>0$ and $t_0\ge3$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dens}
\sum_{\substack{p\le t\\ \rho(p)\ge2}}\frac{w(p)}{p}\ \ge\ \beta\log\log t\quad(t\ge t_0),\qquad\qquad
\sum_{p\le t}\frac{w(p)}{p}\ \le\ \log\log t+\gamma\quad(t\ge3).
\end{equation}
Then there are $c=c(\beta)>0$ and $x_1=x_1(\beta,\gamma,C_0,t_0)$ such that for $x\ge x_1$
\[
\sum_{\substack{q\le x\\ q\in Q}}w(q)\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_q)\ \le\ x\,(\log x)^{-c}.
\]
One can take $c=\beta(1-2^{-1/2})/4$. The constants do not depend on the sets $A_p$, only on their sizes
through~\eqref{eq:dens}. (By the second condition, $S_w(x)=\sum_{q\le x,\,q\in Q}w(q)\ll_{\gamma,C_0}x$, so the bound
is a saving of $(\log x)^{-c}$ over the trivial one whenever $w$ has positive mean value.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We follow \cite[\S\S3--4]{KowalskiSoundararajan2021} and indicate the changes. Write
$W(h;q)=\rho(q)^{-1}\sum_{a\in A_q}\e(ha/q)$ for the normalised Weyl sums. Two facts from
\cite[Lemma~3.5, Lemma~3.7]{KowalskiSoundararajan2021} hold for every family $(A_p)$, with no hypothesis on
the sets beyond their sizes: (a) for coprime $q_1,q_2$, $W(h;q_1q_2)=W(\bar q_1h;q_2)W(\bar q_2h;q_1)$ with
$q_1\bar q_1\equiv1\ (q_2)$, $q_2\bar q_2\equiv1\ (q_1)$; (b) for $q\in Q$ and $L\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L37}
\frac1q\sum_{a\bmod q}\ \sum_{0<|h|\le L}\frac{|W(ah;q)|}{|h|}\ \ll\ (\log L)\prod_{p\mid q}\Bigl(\frac1{\sqrt{\rho(p)}}+\frac1{\sqrt p}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
which is their Lemma~3.7 in dimension one, where the hyperplane constant $\lambda(p)$ equals $1$ (a point).
Also, by the Erd\H os--Tur\'an inequality, $\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_q)\ll L^{-1}+\sum_{0<|h|\le L}|W(h;q)|/|h|$.

Throughout, $\prod_{p\le t}(1+w(p)/p)\le\exp(\sum_{p\le t}w(p)/p)\le e^{\gamma}\log t$ by~\eqref{eq:dens}, and
$\sum_{t_1<p\le t_2}w(p)/p\le C_0\sum_{t_1<p\le t_2}1/p\le C_0\log(\log t_2/\log t_1)+O(C_0)$ by Mertens.

Let $P=\sum_{p\le x,\ \rho(p)\ge2}w(p)(1-\rho(p)^{-1/2})/p$, so that $\beta(1-2^{-1/2})\log\log x\le P\le\log\log x+\gamma$
by~\eqref{eq:dens}, and put $z=x^{1/P}$, so that $\log z=\log x/P\ge\log x/(2\log\log x)$ for $x\ge x_1$. Factor
$q=rs$ with $s$ the $z$-smooth and $r$ the $z$-rough part; $w(q)=w(r)w(s)$.

\emph{Case $s\le x^{1/3}$.} Fix $s$ and $L$. By (a) and the Erd\H os--Tur\'an inequality, for $r$ coprime to $s$,
$\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_{rs})\ll L^{-1}+\sum_{0<|h|\le L}|W(\bar rh;s)|/|h|$, where we bounded $|W(\bar sh;r)|\le1$. Group the $r$
by the class $a\equiv\bar r\pmod s$: then
\[
\sum_{\substack{r\le x/s,\ rs\in Q\\ p\mid r\Rightarrow p>z}}w(r)\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_{rs})\ \ll\ \sum_{\substack{a\bmod s\\ (a,s)=1}}\Bigl(\frac1L+\sum_{0<|h|\le L}\frac{|W(ah;s)|}{|h|}\Bigr)\ \sum_{\substack{r\le x/s,\ r\equiv\bar a\ (s)\\ p\mid r\Rightarrow p>z}}w(r).
\]
This is where the first change occurs. The inner sum is a sum of the non-negative multiplicative function
$w\cdot\mathbf 1_{\text{$z$-rough}}$ (which is $\le C_0^{\omega(n)}\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon$) over a progression to the modulus
$s\le x^{1/3}\le(x/s)^{1/2}$; by Shiu's theorem~\cite{Shiu1980}, uniformly in $a$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{r\le x/s,\ r\equiv\bar a\ (s)\\ p\mid r\Rightarrow p>z}}w(r)\ \ll\ \frac{x/s}{\varphi(s)\log(x/s)}\exp\Bigl(\sum_{z<p\le x}\frac{w(p)}p\Bigr)\ \ll\ \frac{x}{s\,\varphi(s)\log x}\,P^{C_0},
\]
since $\sum_{z<p\le x}w(p)/p\le C_0\log P+O(C_0)$. Inserting~\eqref{eq:L37},
\[
\sum_{\substack{r\le x/s\\ rs\in Q,\ r\ \mathrm{rough}}}w(r)\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_{rs})\ \ll\ \frac{xP^{C_0}}{\varphi(s)\log x}\Bigl(\frac1L+(\log L)\prod_{p\mid s}\Bigl(\frac1{\sqrt{\rho(p)}}+\frac1{\sqrt p}\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Now multiply by $w(s)$ and sum over $z$-smooth $s\le x^{1/3}$, $s\in Q$. Since $w(s)/\varphi(s)=\prod_{p\mid s}w(p)/(p-1)$,
\[
\sum_{s}\frac{w(s)}{\varphi(s)}\prod_{p\mid s}\Bigl(\frac1{\sqrt{\rho(p)}}+\frac1{\sqrt p}\Bigr)\le\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\ p\in Q}}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}{p-1}\Bigl(\frac1{\sqrt{\rho(p)}}+\frac1{\sqrt p}\Bigr)\Bigr)
\ll\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\ p\in Q}}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}{p}\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{\substack{p\le z\\ \rho(p)\ge2}}\frac{w(p)}p\Bigl(1-\frac1{\sqrt{\rho(p)}}\Bigr)\Bigr),
\]
because $(1+w(1/\sqrt\rho+p^{-1/2})/(p-1))/(1+w/p)\le\exp(-w(1-1/\sqrt\rho)/p+O(C_0p^{-3/2}))$ and the primes with
$\rho(p)=1$ contribute $O(1)$; the exponential is $\exp(-P_z)$ with $P_z$ the sum defining $P$ cut at $z$, and
$P-P_z=\sum_{z<p\le x}(\dots)\le C_0\log P+O(C_0)$, so $\exp(-P_z)\ll P^{C_0}e^{-P}$. Similarly
$\sum_s w(s)/\varphi(s)\ll\prod_{p\le z,\ p\in Q}(1+w(p)/p)$. With $L=e^{P}$ the case contributes
\[
\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\prod_{\substack{p\le z\\ p\in Q}}\Bigl(1+\frac{w(p)}p\Bigr)\cdot P^{2C_0+1}e^{-P}\ \ll\ x\,P^{2C_0+1}e^{-P},
\]
using $\prod_{p\le z}(1+w(p)/p)\le e^\gamma\log z\le e^\gamma\log x$.

\emph{Case $s>x^{1/3}$.} Here we use $\mathrm{disc}\le1$ and bound the weighted number of $q\le x$ in $Q$ whose
$z$-smooth part exceeds $x^{1/3}$. For $X\ge1$ let $\mathcal R(X)=\sum_{r\le X,\ p\mid r\Rightarrow p>z}w(r)$. If $X<z$ then
$\mathcal R(X)=1$. If $X\ge z$, the upper bound of Halberstam and Richert for non-negative multiplicative
functions~\cite[Theorem~III.3.5]{Tenenbaum2015} gives
$\mathcal R(X)\ll_{C_0}(X/\log X)\prod_{z<p\le X}(1+w(p)/p)\ll(X/\log z)P^{C_0}=XP^{C_0+1}/\log x$. In both cases
$\mathcal R(X)\le1+O(XP^{C_0+1}/\log x)$, and for $X=x/s\ge1$ this is $\le2x/s$ once $x\ge x_1$. Hence
\[
\sum_{\substack{q\le x,\ q\in Q\\ s>x^{1/3}}}w(q)\ \le\ \sum_{\substack{x^{1/3}<s\le x\\ P^+(s)\le z}}w(s)\,\mathcal R(x/s)\ \le\ 2x\sum_{\substack{s>x^{1/3}\\ P^+(s)\le z}}\frac{w(s)}{s}.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:rankin} with $X=x^{1/3}$, so $u=\log X/\log z=P/3$; the conditions $z\ge z_0$ and
$u_0\le u\le\log z$ hold for $x\ge x_1(\beta,\gamma,C_0,t_0)$, because $P\to\infty$ and $P/3\le\log\log x\le\log z$. The sum is
therefore $\le e^{\gamma}\log z\cdot\exp(-\tfrac16P\log(P/3))$, and since $\log z\le\log x=e^{\log\log x}\le e^{P/(\beta(1-2^{-1/2}))}$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{q\le x,\ q\in Q\\ s>x^{1/3}}}w(q)\ \ll_\gamma\ x\exp\Bigl(\frac{P}{\beta(1-2^{-1/2})}-\frac{P\log(P/3)}6\Bigr)\ \le\ x\,e^{-P}
\]
as soon as $\log(P/3)\ge6(1+1/(\beta(1-2^{-1/2})))$, which again holds for $x\ge x_1$. This is the second change: the
Dickman-type decay of Lemma~\ref{lem:rankin}, with $u\asymp\log\log x$, beats every fixed power of $\log x$, so no
analogue of the counting argument of \cite[Lemma~3.2]{KowalskiSoundararajan2021} is needed.

\emph{Conclusion.} Adding the two cases, $\sum_{q\le x,\ q\in Q}w(q)\mathrm{disc}(\Delta_q)\ll_{\gamma,C_0}x\,P^{2C_0+1}e^{-P}$, and
since $P\ge\beta(1-2^{-1/2})\log\log x$, this is $\le x(\log x)^{-c}$ with $c=\beta(1-2^{-1/2})/4$ for $x\ge x_1$.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} Kowalski and Soundararajan's theorem says that a set defined prime by prime through the
Chinese remainder theorem is equidistributed modulo most $q$, at a rate governed only by how many primes carry
at least two residues; the proof averages the rough part of the modulus over residue classes modulo the smooth
part, and the local sets enter only through their sizes. Multiplicative weights of bounded mean ride along:
Shiu's theorem replaces the sieve count, Rankin's trick with Dickman decay disposes of the moduli with a large
smooth part, and the rest is bookkeeping in Euler products.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:KSwconst}
For $w=\lambda\omega$ and the split primes of a quadratic $f$: $w(p)=2p/(p-4)\in[2,10]$ for split $p\ge5$, $w(p)=0$ at inert
primes and at the finitely many primes dividing $2D$, $\rho(p)=2$ at split primes, and~\eqref{eq:dens} holds with
$\beta=1-o(1)$ and some $\gamma=\gamma(f)$ by the prime number theorem for the character $\chi_D$
($\sum_{p\le t\ \mathrm{split}}1/p=\tfrac12\log\log t+O_D(1)$). Removing in addition the primes dividing a squarefree $u$
(where $R^{(u)}_p=\emptyset$) lowers the first sum in~\eqref{eq:dens} by at most
$C_0\sum_{p\mid u}1/p\le C_0(\log\log\log u+O(1))$ and can only lower the second; so~\eqref{eq:dens} holds with
$\beta=\tfrac12$, the same $\gamma$, and $t_0=\exp((\log\log u)^{A})$ for an absolute $A=A(C_0)$, $A\le3$ for
$C_0\le10$: in particular uniformly for $t\ge\exp((\log\log u)^3)$, $u\ge u_1$. The resulting exponent is $c=(1-2^{-1/2})/8\approx0.037$; no attempt has been made to
optimise it, and none is needed for the Ces\`aro form.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[small moduli, unconditional]\label{thm:small}
For every irreducible quadratic $f$ there is $c=c(f)>0$ such that, uniformly for squarefree $u$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$
and $Y\ge\max(\exp((\log\log u)^3),Y_1(f))$,
\[
\mathcal P^{\le}_u(Y)=\sum_{d'\le Y}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})\ll_fY(\log Y)^{-c}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, $\sum_{u\le H^{1-\varepsilon}}w(u)\,\mathcal P^{\le}_u(H/u)\ll_{f,\varepsilon}H(\log H)^{1-c}$: the
small-moduli part of the pieces with $u\le H^{1-\varepsilon}$ is $o(H\log H)$ unconditionally (the pieces with
$u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$ are treated in full in Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:koksma}, $|\mathcal P^\le_u(Y)|\le\tfrac32\sum_{d'\le Y}\lambda(d')\omega(d')\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta^{(u)}_{d'})$, the sum over
squarefree split $d'$ coprime to $u$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:KSw} to the family $A_p=R^{(u)}_p$ (of size $2$ at
split $p\nmid 2Du$, empty otherwise) and to $w=|\lambda\omega|\cdot\mathbf 1_{(\cdot,2Du)=1}$ (which is non-negative, multiplicative,
and bounded by $10$ at every prime, $|\lambda\omega(3)|=6$); by Remark~\ref{rem:KSwconst} the
hypotheses hold with constants depending only on $f$ provided $Y\ge\exp((\log\log u)^3)$ and $u\ge u_1$, and for the
finitely many $u<u_1$ they hold with $t_0=t_0(f)$. Since $Q$ is then exactly the set of admissible $d'$ and
$\rho(d')=\omega(d')$, the bound is $\ll Y(\log Y)^{-c}$ with $c=c(f)$. For the consequence, the weight
$w(u)$ has $\sum_{u\le U}w(u)/u\ll\log U$, and for $u\le H^{1-\varepsilon}$ we have $\log(H/u)\ge\varepsilon\log H$, so
$\sum_{u\le H^{1-\varepsilon}}w(u)\,(H/u)(\log(H/u))^{-c}\ll_\varepsilon H(\log H)^{-c}\sum_{u\le H}w(u)/u\ll H(\log H)^{1-c}$.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} For moduli shorter than the length there is no short-interval problem, only the question
whether the dilated roots are spread out modulo $d'$; and spreading out is a consequence of the Chinese
remainder theorem alone, as Kowalski and Soundararajan showed, at a rate that does not see which two-point
sets sit at the primes. So this half of every piece is unconditional, and the loss against the trivial bound
is a power of $\log$, which is exactly what the Ces\`aro form can afford.

\subsection{Large moduli: the far moduli are trivial}\label{sec:far}

For $d'>Y$ and $m\in[1,d']$ at most one $h\le Y$ lies in the class of $m$, so $B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=(Y-m)^+-Y^2/(2d')$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pgt}
\sum_{d'>Z}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R'_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})=\sum_{x\le Y}(Y-x)\sum_{\substack{d'>Z\\ d'\mid Q_u(x)}}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\;-\;\frac{Y^2}2\sum_{d'>Z}\frac{\lambda(d')\rho(d')}{d'^2}=:\mathcal A_Z-\mathcal E_Z\qquad(Z\ge Y),
\end{equation}
where $d'$ ranges over squarefree split moduli coprime to $u$, and we used that an integer $x\in[1,Y]$ is the
representative of an element of $R'_{d'}$ exactly when $d'\mid Q_u(x)$. Both terms are of the same size, and
the question is how well they cancel. The next lemma says that for $Z=u^2Y$ there is nothing to cancel: both
terms are already small.

\begin{lemma}[far moduli]\label{lem:far}
Let $u$ be squarefree with $\omega(u)\ge1$, $Y\ge4|D|+4$ and $L\ge1$. Then
\[
\mathcal P^{\mathrm{far}}_u(Y;L):=\sum_{d'>u^2YL}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})\ \ll_f\ \frac Y{u^2L},
\]
uniformly in $u$ and $L$. Consequently $\sum_{u\ge1}w(u)\,|\mathcal P^{\mathrm{far}}_u(H/u;L)|\ll_fH/L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $Z=u^2YL$. Since $\lambda\rho$ has mean value $\ll1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces}), $\sum_{d'>Z}\lambda\rho(d')/d'^2\ll1/Z$
and $\mathcal E_Z\ll Y^2/Z=Y/(u^2L)$.

For $\mathcal A_Z$ write $\lambda=1*\kappa$ with $\kappa$ multiplicative, supported on squarefree split integers,
$\kappa(p)=\lambda(p)-1=4/(p-4)\ge0$. Dropping the conditions on the moduli (all terms are non-negative) and
substituting $d'=ge$... more precisely writing $d'=g\,d''$ with $g\mid d'$ carrying $\kappa$, and $d''=n/(ge)$ with
$n=Q_u(x)$, so that $1/d''=ge/n$ and $d''>Z/g$ becomes $e<n/(gZ)$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{d'>Z\\ d'\mid n}}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\le\sum_{g\mid n}\kappa(g)\sum_{\substack{e\mid n/g\\ e<n/(gZ)}}\frac{e}{n},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\mathcal A_Z\le\sum_g\kappa(g)\sum_e e\sum_{\substack{x\le Y,\ ge\mid Q_u(x)\\ Q_u(x)>geZ}}\frac{Y-x}{Q_u(x)}.
\]
In the inner sum $Q_u(x)>geu^2YL$ and $Q_u(x)\le u^2Y^2+|D|\le2u^2Y^2$, so $ge<2Y/L$; moreover
$u^2x^2=Q_u(x)+D>geu^2YL-|D|\ge geu^2YL/2$ because $Y\ge2|D|$, so $x>x_0:=\sqrt{geYL/2}$, and then
$u^2x^2\ge2|D|$ gives $Q_u(x)\ge u^2x^2/2$. Therefore
\[
\frac{Y-x}{Q_u(x)}\le\min\Bigl(\frac1{geu^2},\ \frac{2Y}{u^2x^2}\Bigr).
\]
The $x$ with $ge\mid Q_u(x)$ lie in $\rho_u(ge)$ residue classes modulo $ge$, where $\rho_u(m)$ denotes the number of
roots of $Q_u$ modulo $m$ (for all $m$, not only squarefree split ones). In one class, the first term beyond $x_0$
contributes at most $2Y/(u^2x_0^2)=4/(geu^2L)$ and the others at most $(ge)^{-1}\int_{x_0}^\infty2Y\,dx/(u^2x^2)=2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{Y/(geL)}/(geu^2)$;
so
\[
\mathcal A_Z\le\frac1{u^2}\sum_g\frac{\kappa(g)}g\sum_{e<2Y/(gL)}\rho_u(ge)\Bigl(\frac4L+3\sqrt{\frac Y{geL}}\Bigr).
\]
Now $\rho_u$ is multiplicative; $\rho_u(p^k)=1+\chi_D(p)\le2$ for $p\nmid2Du$; $\rho_u(p^k)=0$ for $p\mid u$, $p\nmid D$; and
for the finitely many $p\mid2D$, $\rho_u(p^k)\le4p^{v_p(D)+2}$ uniformly in $k$ and in squarefree $u$ (if $k>v_p(D)+2$ a
solution forces $v_p(u^2x^2)=v_p(D)$, which fixes $v_p(x)$ and leaves a congruence $x'^2\equiv D'\ (p^{k-v_p(D)})$ with
$D'$ a unit, having at most $4$ solutions, each lifting to $p^{v_p(D)/2+1}$ classes). Hence $\rho_u(ge)\le\rho_u(g)\rho_u(e)$
for squarefree split $g$, and $\sum_{e\le t}\rho_u(e)\ll_ft$ uniformly in $u$ (the Dirichlet series of $\rho_u$ is
$\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_D)$ times a product absolutely convergent for $\Re s>\tfrac12$ with constants depending only on $f$),
whence $\sum_{e\le t}\rho_u(e)e^{-1/2}\ll_ft^{1/2}$ by partial summation. With $t=2Y/(gL)$ the inner sum is therefore
$\ll\rho_u(g)\bigl(Y/(gL^2)+Y/(gL)\bigr)\ll\rho_u(g)Y/(gL)$, and $\mathcal A_Z\ll(Y/(u^2L))\sum_g\kappa(g)\rho_u(g)/g^2\ll Y/(u^2L)$.

For the consequence, $\sum_{u\ge1}w(u)(H/u)u^{-2}L^{-1}=HL^{-1}\sum_uw(u)u^{-3}\ll H/L$, as $\sum_{u\le t}w(u)\ll t$.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} When the modulus exceeds $u^2$ times the length, each root that falls below the length has
a complementary divisor $e=Q_u(x)/d'$ below the length, and the weights $1/d'=e/Q_u(x)$ decay like $1/x^2$;
counting crudely, the whole range beyond $u^2YL$ weighs only $Y/(u^2L)$, which is summable over $u$ and small
for $L\to\infty$. So the far moduli are negligible for trivial reasons, at the price of extending the window to
$u^2YL$; no reflection argument is needed. The trivial bound also fixes the scale of everything else: the small
moduli $d'\le Y$ have trivial size $\asymp Y$, the window $Y<d'\le u^2YL$ has $\asymp Y\log(u^2L)$, the far moduli
$Y/(u^2L)$.

\section{The first piece and the large pieces}\label{sec:unconditional}

\subsection{$u=1$: the theorem of Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec}

\begin{lemma}[sharp cut-offs from smooth ones]\label{lem:sharp}
Let $D$ be a fundamental discriminant for which Theorem~1.1 of~\cite{DFI2012} holds (positive odd $D$ as
stated there; negative $D$ by their \S16), and let $q\ge1$, $k\ge1$. Then for $t\ge2$,
\[
\sum_{\substack{d\le t\\ q\mid d}}\ \sum_{r\in R_d}\e\Bigl(\frac{kr}d\Bigr)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}k^{1/4}\,t^{11/12+\varepsilon},
\]
uniformly in $q$; the same holds with the additional factor $\lambda(d)$, and with $d$ restricted to squarefree split
moduli.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~1.1 of~\cite{DFI2012} states: for $f$ smooth, supported on $[V,2V]$, with $|f|\le1$ and $y^2|f''(y)|\le1$,
$\sum_{c\equiv0(q)}f(c)\sum_{b^2\equiv D(c)}\e(hb/c)\ll h^{1/4}(V+h\sqrt D)^{3/4}D^{1/8-1/1331}$ with an absolute
implied constant. The statement is linear in $f$; hence if $f$ satisfies $|f|\le\Delta^2$, $y^2|f''|\le\Delta^2$, the
bound holds with an extra factor $\Delta^2$ (apply it to $f/\Delta^2$). Let $\Delta=t^{1/12}$ and let $\chi_t$ be a smooth
function equal to $1$ on $[0,t(1-1/\Delta)]$, to $0$ on $[t,\infty)$, with $|\chi_t^{(j)}|\ll(\Delta/t)^j$. Decompose
$\chi_t=\sum_V\chi_t\psi_V$ with a smooth dyadic partition of unity $\{\psi_V\}$ on $[1,\infty)$: each piece is supported
on $[V,2V]$ with $y^2|(\chi_t\psi_V)''|\ll1$ except the piece containing the edge, for which $y^2|\cdot''|\ll\Delta^2$.
Summing the DFI bound over the dyadic $V\le t$ gives $\ll\Delta^2k^{1/4}t^{3/4+\varepsilon}$ for fixed $D$. The difference
between the sharp cut-off and $\chi_t$ is supported on $(t(1-1/\Delta),t]$ and is bounded, for the moduli $q\mid d$
there, by $\sum_{d\in(t(1-1/\Delta),t],\ q\mid d}\omega(d)\ll(t/(q\Delta)+1)\,t^{\varepsilon}$ by Shiu's theorem for the
multiplicative function $\omega$ on short intervals of progressions ($t/\Delta\ge t^{\varepsilon}$). Altogether
$\ll t^{1/6}k^{1/4}t^{3/4+\varepsilon}+t^{11/12+\varepsilon}\ll k^{1/4}t^{11/12+\varepsilon}$. The weight $\lambda(d)=\sum_{g\mid d}\kappa(g)$
is removed by writing $\sum_d\lambda(d)F(d)=\sum_g\kappa(g)\sum_{g\mid d}F(d)$, applying the bound with $q$ replaced by
$\mathrm{lcm}(q,g)$, and using $\sum_g\kappa(g)<\infty$; the restriction to squarefree split $d$ is a further M\"obius
inversion (over $\ell^2\mid d$ and over the inert and special primes, which carry no roots or a single root and
are removed by inclusion--exclusion at the cost of the divisor bound).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[the first piece: small moduli]\label{thm:u1}
For $D$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp}, $\mathcal P^{\le}_1(Y)=\sum_{d'\le Y}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})\ll_{D,\varepsilon}Y^{12/13+\varepsilon}$.
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:far} ($u=1$, $L=Y^{\varepsilon}$; the window $(Y,YL]$ is then the only part not
covered) this gives $\mathcal P_1(Y)=\mathcal W_1(Y;Y^{\varepsilon})+O(Y^{1-\varepsilon})$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:windowW}
with $(\theta,B)=(\tfrac{11}{12},\tfrac14)$ bounds the window by $Y^{11/12+O(\varepsilon)}$: so $\mathcal P_1(Y)\ll Y^{1-\delta}$
unconditionally.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp} gives~\eqref{eq:W} for $u=1$ with $\theta=\tfrac{11}{12}+\varepsilon$, $B=\tfrac14$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:partial}, $T_k(Y)\ll k^{1/4}(kY)^{12/13+\varepsilon}$, and inserting into~\eqref{eq:Ple}:
$\sum_{k\le K}k^{-2}T_k\ll Y^{12/13+\varepsilon}\sum_{k\le K}k^{-2+1/4+12/13}\ll Y^{12/13+\varepsilon}K^{0.18}$, while
$\sum_{k>K}k^{-2}|T_k|\ll Y/K$; with $K=Y^{1/20}$ both are $\ll Y^{12/13+\varepsilon}$.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Large $u$: averaging the dilations}\label{sec:typeII}

Throughout this subsection $g=w/c_f$ is the multiplicative function of the set-up ($g(p)=\lambda(p)\omega(p)$ for odd
$p\nmid D$, $g$ supported on squarefree integers coprime to $D$), $\chi=\chi_D$ is the Kronecker symbol, and $m$, $m_d$
are the constants
\begin{equation}\label{eq:md}
m=L(1,\chi)\prod_p\Bigl(1-\frac1p\Bigr)\Bigl(1-\frac{\chi(p)}p\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\frac{g(p)}p\Bigr),\qquad
m_d=m\prod_{p\mid d}\Bigl(1+\frac{g(p)}p\Bigr)^{-1},\qquad M_d=c_fm_d,
\end{equation}
the product converging absolutely since $g(p)-1-\chi(p)=(1+\chi(p))\kappa(p)\ll1/p$ for $p\nmid2D$.

\begin{lemma}[$\lambda\omega$ in progressions and against characters]\label{lem:AP}
For $U\ge2$ and $d\ge1$ coprime to $2D$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{n\le U\\ (n,d)=1}}g(n)=m_dU+O_{D,\varepsilon}\bigl(U^{1/2+\varepsilon}d^{\varepsilon}\bigr)$;
\item[(ii)] for every non-principal character $\psi$ modulo $d$, $\displaystyle\sum_{n\le U}g(n)\psi(n)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}U^{1/2+\varepsilon}d^{1/2}$;
\item[(iii)] for every $b$ coprime to $d$, $\displaystyle\sum_{\substack{n\le U\\ n\equiv b\ (d)}}g(n)=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\substack{n\le U\\ (n,d)=1}}g(n)+O_{D,\varepsilon}\bigl(U^{1/2+\varepsilon}d^{1/2}\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(ii) The Dirichlet series of $g\psi$ is $\prod_{p\nmid2D}(1+g(p)\psi(p)p^{-s})=L(s,\psi)L(s,\chi\psi)G_\psi(s)$ with
$G_\psi(s)=\prod_p(1+g(p)\psi(p)p^{-s})(1-\psi(p)p^{-s})(1-\chi\psi(p)p^{-s})$, an Euler product of the form
$\prod_p(1+O(p^{-s-1})+O(p^{-2s}))$, absolutely convergent for $\Re s>\tfrac12$ uniformly in $\psi$; write $\gamma_\psi$ for
its coefficients, so that $\sum_n|\gamma_\psi(n)|n^{-1/2-\varepsilon}\ll_\varepsilon1$. Then
$g\psi=\psi*\chi\psi*\gamma_\psi$ and
\[
\sum_{n\le U}g(n)\psi(n)=\sum_{n_3}\gamma_\psi(n_3)\sum_{n_1n_2\le U/n_3}\psi(n_1)\,\chi\psi(n_2).
\]
Both $\psi$ and $\chi\psi$ are non-principal characters (to the moduli $d$ and $d|D|$; $\chi\psi$ is non-principal
because $\chi$ is primitive of conductor $|D|$ coprime to $d$), so by the P\'olya--Vinogradov inequality their partial
sums are $\ll(d|D|)^{1/2}\log(d|D|)$, and Dirichlet's hyperbola method gives
$\sum_{n_1n_2\le X}\psi(n_1)\chi\psi(n_2)\ll X^{1/2}d^{1/2}\log(d|D|)$. Summing over $n_3$ with the weights
$|\gamma_\psi(n_3)|(U/n_3)^{1/2}$ gives (ii).

(i) Likewise $\sum_{(n,d)=1}g(n)n^{-s}=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)G_d(s)$ with $G_d(s)=\prod_p(1+g(p)\mathbf 1_{p\nmid d}p^{-s})(1-p^{-s})(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})$,
whose coefficients $\gamma_d$ satisfy $\sum_n|\gamma_d(n)|n^{-1/2-\varepsilon}\ll d^{\varepsilon}$ (the factors at $p\mid d$ are
$(1-p^{-s})(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})$). Since $\sum_{n_1n_2\le X}\chi(n_2)=L(1,\chi)X+O(X^{1/2})$ by the hyperbola method,
$\sum_{n\le U,(n,d)=1}g(n)=\sum_{n_3}\gamma_d(n_3)\bigl(L(1,\chi)U/n_3+O((U/n_3)^{1/2})\bigr)=L(1,\chi)G_d(1)U+O(U^{1/2+\varepsilon}d^\varepsilon)$,
and $L(1,\chi)G_d(1)=m_d$. (iii) follows from (i) and (ii) by orthogonality of characters.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} The number of roots of $f$ modulo $n$ is a divisor-type function twisted by a character, and
such functions are equidistributed in progressions to moduli well below $U$; the proof is Dirichlet's hyperbola
method with the P\'olya--Vinogradov inequality. No deep input is used.

\paragraph{The dilation average.} For squarefree split $d'>1$, a unit $a$ modulo $d'$ and $Y>0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
G_{d'}(a,Y)=\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep{ar}{d'}),\qquad
\bar G_{d'}(Y)=\frac1{\varphi(d')}\sum_{a\in(\Z/d')^\times}G_{d'}(a,Y),
\end{equation}
so that the inner sum in the piece~\eqref{eq:piece} is $G_{d'}(\bar u,Y)$ (by~\eqref{eq:m} and the symmetry of $R_{d'}$).

\begin{lemma}[the dilation average]\label{lem:Gbar}
With $\phi(\theta)=\theta-\theta^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gbar}
\bar G_{d'}(Y)=\frac{\omega(d')}{2\varphi(d')}\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)\,e\,\phi\Bigl(\Bigl\{\frac Ye\Bigr\}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Consequently: (a) $|\bar G_{d'}(Y)|\le\omega(d')\sigma(d')/(8\varphi(d'))$ and $|\bar G_{d'}(Y)|\le\omega(d')\tau(d')Y/(2\varphi(d'))$ for all $Y>0$;
(b) $\bar G_{d'}(Y)=-\omega(d')Y^2/(2d')=G_{d'}(a,Y)$ for $0<Y<1$ and every $a$;
(c) $Y\mapsto G_{d'}(a,Y)$ and $Y\mapsto\bar G_{d'}(Y)$ are continuous, piecewise linear, with $|\partial_Y|\le\omega(d')$;
(d) $|G_{d'}(a,Y)|\le\omega(d')\min(d',Y)$ for all $a$, $Y$;
(e) $\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\bar G_{d'}(Y)\frac{dY}{Y^2}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\omega(p)\log p}{2\varphi(p)}=-\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}&\text{if }d'=p\text{ is prime},\\[2ex] 0&\text{if $d'$ has at least two prime factors,}\end{cases}$
the integral converging absolutely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every $r\in R_{d'}$ is a unit ($r^2\equiv D$, $(D,d')=1$), so $a\mapsto ar$ permutes the units and
$\sum_aG_{d'}(a,Y)=\omega(d')\sum_{x\in(\Z/d')^\times}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(x)$. By M\"obius inversion and the elementary identity
$\sum_{h'\le X}(X-h')=X^2/2-X/2+\phi(\{X\})/2$ ($X\ge0$),
\[
\sum_{x\in(\Z/d')^\times}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(x)=\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)\,e\sum_{h'\le Y/e}\Bigl(\frac Ye-h'\Bigr)-\frac{\varphi(d')Y^2}{2d'}
=\frac{Y^2}2\sum_{e\mid d'}\frac{\mu(e)}e-\frac Y2\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)+\frac12\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)e\,\phi\Bigl(\Bigl\{\frac Ye\Bigr\}\Bigr)-\frac{\varphi(d')Y^2}{2d'},
\]
and the first, second and last terms cancel ($d'>1$). This is~\eqref{eq:Gbar}. (a): $0\le e\phi(\{Y/e\})\le e/4$
gives the first bound; for $e\le Y$ also $e\phi\le Y/4$, and for $e>Y$, $e\phi(Y/e)=Y-Y^2/e\in[0,Y]$, giving the
second. (b): for $Y<1$ all $e>Y$, so the sum is $Y\sum\mu(e)-Y^2\sum\mu(e)/e=-Y^2\varphi(d')/d'$; and no root has
representative $\le Y<1$, so $G_{d'}(a,Y)=-\omega(d')Y^2/(2d')$ directly. (c): $\partial_YB^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=\#\{h\le Y:h\equiv m\}-Y/d'\in(-1,1)$
away from the integers. (d): from the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces} for $d'\le Y$ (symmetric pairs
contribute $\le d'/4$ each); for $d'>Y$, $B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)+B^{(Y)}_{d'}(d'-m)=(Y-m)^++(Y-d'+m)^+-Y^2/d'\in[-Y,Y]$.
(e): put $K(\tau)=\int_\tau^\infty\phi(\{v\})v^{-2}dv$, so that for $0<T<1$,
$\int_T^\infty\bar G_{d'}(Y)Y^{-2}dY=\frac{\omega}{2\varphi}\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)K(T/e)$, and for $\tau<1$,
$K(\tau)=\int_\tau^1(v^{-1}-1)\,dv+K(1)=-\log\tau-1+\tau+K(1)$. Hence
$\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)K(T/e)=\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)\log e+T\varphi(d')/d'$, because $\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)=0$; letting $T\to0$
(absolute convergence at $0$ follows from (b)) gives $\frac{\omega}{2\varphi}\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)\log e$, and for
$d'=p_1\cdots p_k$, $\sum_{e\mid d'}\mu(e)\log e=-\sum_i\log p_i\sum_{e'\mid d'/p_i}\mu(e')$ is $-\log d'$ if $k=1$ and $0$ if $k\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Type II range]\label{thm:typeII}
For every $\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac1{10})$ there is $\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0$ such that
\[
\sum_{u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\,\mathcal P_u(H/u)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)\,H+O_{f,\varepsilon}\bigl(H^{1-\delta}\bigr),
\]
the sum over all squarefree $u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$, including $u>H$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coff}
c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)=\sum_{d'}\frac{\lambda(d')M_{d'}}{d'}\int_0^\infty\bar G_{d'}(Y)\frac{dY}{Y^2}
=-\sum_{\substack{p\ \mathrm{split}\\ p\nmid2D}}\frac{\lambda(p)\,M_p\,\log p}{p\,(p-1)}\ <\ 0,
\end{equation}
with $M_p=c_fm_p$ as in~\eqref{eq:md}; both series converge absolutely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $U_0=H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$, $Y_0=H/U_0=H^{1/3-\varepsilon}$, $\eta=\varepsilon/2$, and let $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/12$ be the
exponent in Lemma~\ref{lem:AP}. All implied constants depend on $f$ and $\varepsilon$.

\emph{Step 1: $u>H$.} Here $Y=H/u<1$ and $\mathcal P_u(Y)=\sum_{(d',u)=1}(\lambda(d')/d')\bar G_{d'}(Y)$ exactly, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(b).

\emph{Step 2: dyadic blocks.} For $U\in[U_0,H)$ of the form $U_02^j$ consider the $u\in(U,2U]$, $u\le H$, and put
$Y_U=H/U\in(1,Y_0]$ and $Z_U=Y_UH^{\eta}$; for $u$ in the block, $H/u\in[Y_U/2,Y_U)$.

\emph{Step 3: moduli $d'>Z_U$ in a block.} For $d'>Z_U\ge Y$, $G_{d'}(\bar u,Y)=\sum_{x\le Y,\ d'\mid Q_u(x)}(Y-x)-\omega(d')Y^2/(2d')$
as in~\eqref{eq:Pgt}. Since $\lambda(d')\ll d'^{\varepsilon'}$ and $\tau(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon'}$, for $n=Q_u(x)\le2H^2$ we have
$\sum_{d'>Z,\ d'\mid n}\lambda(d')/d'\ll H^{3\varepsilon'}/Z$, so that
$\sum_{d'>Z_U}(\lambda(d')/d')|G_{d'}(\bar u,H/u)|\ll Y_U^2H^{3\varepsilon'}/Z_U$; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(a),
$\sum_{d'>Z_U}(\lambda(d')/d')|\bar G_{d'}(H/u)|\ll Y_U\sum_{d'>Z_U}\lambda\omega\tau(d')/(d'\varphi(d'))\ll Y_UZ_U^{-1+\varepsilon'}$.
Summing either bound over the block with $\sum_{u\le2U}w(u)\ll U$ gives $\ll UY_U^2H^{3\varepsilon'}/Z_U=H^{1-\eta+3\varepsilon'}$.

\emph{Step 4: moduli $d'\le Z_U$ in a block: the averaging.} Fix such a $d'$ and put $G^\circ(a,Y)=G_{d'}(a,Y)-\bar G_{d'}(Y)$,
a function on $(\Z/d')^\times$ with mean zero, so that $G^\circ(a,Y)=\sum_{\psi\ne\psi_0}\hat G(\psi,Y)\psi(a)$ with
$\hat G(\psi,Y)=\varphi(d')^{-1}\sum_aG^\circ(a,Y)\bar\psi(a)$, the sum over non-principal characters modulo $d'$. Since
$\psi(\bar u)=\bar\psi(u)$,
\[
\mathcal E_{d'}(U):=\sum_{\substack{U<u\le2U\\ (u,d')=1}}w(u)\,G^\circ\Bigl(\bar u,\frac Hu\Bigr)=\sum_{\psi\ne\psi_0}\ \sum_{U<u\le2U}w(u)\bar\psi(u)\,\hat G\Bigl(\psi,\frac Hu\Bigr).
\]
For fixed $\psi$, partial summation against $S_\psi(t)=\sum_{U<u\le t}w(u)\bar\psi(u)\ll U^{1/2+\varepsilon'}d'^{1/2}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:AP}(ii)) gives
$|\sum_uw(u)\bar\psi(u)\hat G(\psi,H/u)|\le\sup_t|S_\psi(t)|\bigl(\sup_u|\hat G(\psi,H/u)|+\mathrm{Var}_{u\in(U,2U]}\hat G(\psi,H/u)\bigr)$.
Now sum over $\psi$ using Cauchy--Schwarz and Parseval on $(\Z/d')^\times$:
\[
\sum_\psi|\hat G(\psi,Y)|\le\varphi(d')^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_\psi|\hat G(\psi,Y)|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}=\Bigl(\sum_a|G^\circ(a,Y)|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\varphi(d')^{1/2}\max_a|G^\circ(a,Y)|\le2\varphi(d')^{1/2}\omega(d')\min(d',Y)
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(d), and in the same way, using (c) and $\partial_u=-(H/u^2)\partial_Y$,
$\sum_\psi\mathrm{Var}_u\hat G(\psi,H/u)\le\int_U^{2U}\varphi(d')^{1/2}\max_a|\partial_uG^\circ(a,H/u)|\,du\le2\varphi(d')^{1/2}\omega(d')\,Y_U$.
Hence $|\mathcal E_{d'}(U)|\ll U^{1/2+\varepsilon'}d'^{1/2}\varphi(d')^{1/2}\omega(d')Y_U$, and
\[
\sum_{d'\le Z_U}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}|\mathcal E_{d'}(U)|\ll U^{1/2+\varepsilon'}Y_UZ_U^{1+\varepsilon'}\ll H^{1/2+\varepsilon'}Y_U^{3/2}H^{\eta+2\varepsilon'}\le H^{1/2+\eta+3\varepsilon'+(3/2)(1/3-\varepsilon)}=H^{1-\varepsilon/4},
\]
using $U=H/Y_U$, $Y_U\le Y_0=H^{1/3-\varepsilon}$, $\eta=\varepsilon/2$ and $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/12$. Together with Step~3
(where $\eta-3\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/4$) and the $O(\log H)$ blocks,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:MH}
\sum_{u>U_0}w(u)\mathcal P_u(H/u)=\mathcal M(H)+O(H^{1-\varepsilon/4}\log H),\qquad
\mathcal M(H):=\sum_{d'}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{\substack{u>U_0\\ (u,d')=1}}w(u)\,\bar G_{d'}\Bigl(\frac Hu\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where the double series converges absolutely: for $u\le H$, Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(a) gives
$\sum_{d'}(\lambda/d')|\bar G_{d'}(Y)|\ll\sum_{d'\le Y}\lambda\omega\sigma/(d'\varphi)+Y\sum_{d'>Y}\lambda\omega\tau/(d'\varphi)\ll Y^{\varepsilon'}$, for $u>H$
it gives $\ll Y^2$, and $\sum_{U_0<u\le H}w(u)(H/u)^{\varepsilon'}+\sum_{u>H}w(u)(H/u)^2<\infty$.

\emph{Step 5: the main term.} For each $d'$ let $A_{d'}(t)=\sum_{u\le t,\ (u,d')=1}w(u)=M_{d'}t+E_{d'}(t)$, so that
$E_{d'}(t)\ll t^{1/2+\varepsilon'}d'^{\varepsilon'}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:AP}(i). Stieltjes integration and the substitution $t=H/Y$ give
\[
\sum_{\substack{u>U_0\\ (u,d')=1}}w(u)\bar G_{d'}(H/u)=\int_{U_0}^\infty\bar G_{d'}(H/t)\,dA_{d'}(t)
=M_{d'}H\int_0^{Y_0}\bar G_{d'}(Y)\frac{dY}{Y^2}+R_{d'},\qquad
R_{d'}=-\bar G_{d'}(Y_0)E_{d'}(U_0)-\int_{U_0}^\infty E_{d'}(t)\,\partial_t\bigl[\bar G_{d'}(H/t)\bigr]dt,
\]
the boundary term at infinity vanishing because $|\bar G_{d'}(H/t)|\le\omega H^2/(2d't^2)$ for $t>H$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(a),(b),(c), $\int_{U_0}^H|\partial_t\bar G_{d'}(H/t)|dt=\int_1^{Y_0}|\partial_Y\bar G_{d'}|dY\le\omega(d')Y_0$ and
$\int_H^\infty t^{1/2+\varepsilon'}\omega H^2/(d't^3)\,dt\ll\omega H^{1/2+\varepsilon'}/d'$, so
$R_{d'}\ll\omega(d')d'^{\varepsilon'}\bigl(\sigma(d')/\varphi(d')+Y_0\bigr)H^{1/2+\varepsilon'}$ and
$\sum_{d'\le H^{1/3}}(\lambda(d')/d')|R_{d'}|\ll H^{1/2+1/3+3\varepsilon'}$. For $d'>H^{1/3}$ we bound the two sides separately:
for $U_0<u\le H$ we have $H/u\le Y_0<d'$, so by (a) and (b)
$\sum_{u>U_0}w(u)|\bar G_{d'}(H/u)|\ll(\omega\tau/\varphi)\bigl(\sum_{U_0<u\le H}w(u)H/u+\sum_{u>H}w(u)H^2/u^2\bigr)\ll(\omega\tau(d')/\varphi(d'))H\log H$,
and $M_{d'}H\int_0^{Y_0}|\bar G_{d'}|Y^{-2}dY\ll(\omega\tau/\varphi)H\log H$ as well; summing $(\lambda/d')\cdot$ these over
$d'>H^{1/3}$ gives $\ll H^{2/3+\varepsilon'}\log H$. Finally the tails of the integrals: by (a), for $d'\le Y_0$,
$\int_{Y_0}^\infty|\bar G_{d'}|Y^{-2}dY\le\sigma/(8Y_0)$, and for $d'>Y_0$ it is
$\le\int_{Y_0}^{d'}\frac{\tau\,dY}{2Y}+\int_{d'}^\infty\frac{\sigma\,dY}{8Y^2}\ll\tau(d')\bigl(1+\log(d'/Y_0)\bigr)$, whence
$\sum_{d'}(\lambda M_{d'}/d')\int_{Y_0}^\infty|\bar G_{d'}|Y^{-2}dY\ll Y_0^{-1}\log Y_0+Y_0^{-1+\varepsilon'}$. The same bounds
with $Y_0$ replaced by $1$, together with (b) below $1$, show that the series defining $c_{\mathrm{off}}$ in~\eqref{eq:coff}
converges absolutely. Altogether $\mathcal M(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H+O(H^{5/6+3\varepsilon'}+H^{2/3+2\varepsilon'}\log H)$, and
with~\eqref{eq:MH} the theorem follows with $\delta=\varepsilon/5$. The second expression for $c_{\mathrm{off}}$ is
Lemma~\ref{lem:Gbar}(e), and it is negative since every term is.
\end{proof}

\emph{In words.} When the agreeing part $u$ is much larger than the length $Y=H/u$, the dilation $\bar u$ runs
through all units modulo $d'$ as $u$ runs through a dyadic range, so the root set may be replaced by its average
over all dilations; this costs the error of Lemma~\ref{lem:AP}, and Parseval on the group of units keeps that cost
to $\sqrt{\varphi(d')}$ rather than $\varphi(d')$ times the error per class, which is what fixes the range
$u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$. The average over dilations is not zero: a root is an integer $\ge1$, and the Ces\`aro weight
$Y-x$ sees the gap between the discrete count and the continuous expectation $Y^2/(2d')$. Integrated over the
length this gap has a remarkably simple value, $-\log p/(p-1)$ for a prime modulus and exactly $0$ for a composite
one, which is where the closed formula~\eqref{eq:coff} comes from. The dilation, which is the obstacle for
small $u$, is the mechanism for large $u$; but it leaves a main term.

\begin{corollary}[the constant, and the mean of the sharp off-diagonal]\label{cor:coff}
Let $\Off_f(t)=\sum_dW_f(d)\sum_{s\ne s'}\psi_d(s'-s,t)$ be the sharp off-diagonal of part~I, $\psi_d(m,t)=\#\{h\le t:h\equiv m\ (d)\}-t/d$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharpcesaro}
\Off^\ast_f(H)=\sum_{t<H}\Off_f(t)-\frac{\kappa_f}2H,\qquad\kappa_f=\sum_dW_f(d)\frac{\omega(d)(\omega(d)-1)}d=1-D_f(1),
\end{equation}
where $D_f(1)=\sum_da_f(d)/d$ is the value at $1$ of the diagonal Dirichlet series of part~I. Consequently, if the
pieces with $u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$ contribute $o(H)$ to $\Off^\ast_f(H)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:A} below gives this under
Hypothesis~W), then $\Off^\ast_f(H)\sim c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H$, the Ces\`aro constant of Conjecture~1 of part~I is the
diagonal constant plus $C(f)^2c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)$, and the sharp off-diagonal $\Off_f(t)$ has mean value
\[
\lim_{H\to\infty}\frac1H\sum_{t\le H}\Off_f(t)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)+\frac{1-D_f(1)}2 .
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For integer $H$, $B^{(H)}_d(m)=\sum_{t<H}\#\{h\le t:h\equiv m\}-H^2/(2d)=\sum_{t<H}\psi_d(m,t)+H(H-1)/(2d)-H^2/(2d)$, and the
number of ordered pairs $s\ne s'$ is $\omega(d)(\omega(d)-1)$; the identity $\sum_dW_f(d)\omega(d)^2/d=1$ is the statement
that $S_f$ has mean value $C(f)^2$ (part~I, Theorem~2), and $\sum_dW_f(d)\omega(d)/d=\sum_da_f(d)/d=D_f(1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{The window, Hypothesis W and Theorem A}\label{sec:W}

\subsection{Where the difficulty sits}

Collecting Sections~\ref{sec:koksma}--\ref{sec:unconditional}: in the piece $u$, the moduli $d'\le Y$ are
unconditional with a logarithmic saving (Theorem~\ref{thm:small}), the moduli $d'>u^2YL$ are $O(Y/(u^2L))$
for trivial reasons (Lemma~\ref{lem:far}), and the pieces with $u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$ are Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII},
main term included. What is left, for $1<u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$, is the \emph{window}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
\mathcal W_u(Y;L)=\sum_{Y<d'\le u^2YL}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})
=\sum_{Y<d'\le u^2YL}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\Bigl[\sum_{\substack{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}\\ \rep x{d'}\le Y}}\bigl(Y-\rep x{d'}\bigr)-\frac{\omega(d')Y^2}{2d'}\Bigr],
\end{equation}
in which the interval $[1,Y]$ covers a fraction $Y/d'\in[(u^2L)^{-1},1)$ of a period. It is empty for $u=1$,
$L=1$, and its trivial size is $\asymp Y\log(u^2L)$. Summarising:

\begin{theorem}[A$'$: unconditional reduction]\label{thm:Aprime}
For every irreducible quadratic $f$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $c=c(f)>0$ such that
\[
\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)\,H+\sum_{u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\,\mathcal W_u\Bigl(\frac Hu;\log H\Bigr)+O_{f,\varepsilon}\bigl(H(\log H)^{1-c}\bigr).
\]
In particular the Ces\`aro form of Hypothesis~(E), $\Off^\ast_f(H)=o(H\log H)$, holds if and only if
$\sum_{u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\mathcal W_u(H/u;\log H)=o(H\log H)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:pieces}, Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} for $u>H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$, and for $u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$
(so $Y=H/u\ge H^{1/3-\varepsilon}$) the split $\mathcal P_u(Y)=\mathcal P^\le_u(Y)+\mathcal W_u(Y;\log H)+\mathcal P^{\mathrm{far}}_u(Y;\log H)$
with Theorem~\ref{thm:small} (applicable since $Y\ge\exp((\log\log u)^3)$) and Lemma~\ref{lem:far}:
$\sum_{u\le H^{2/3+\varepsilon}}w(u)\bigl[(H/u)(\log H)^{-c}+H/(u^3\log H)\bigr]\ll H(\log H)^{1-c}$.
\end{proof}

The window is where the two mechanisms of this paper both fail. Koksma's inequality with the function
$(Y-\theta d')^+$, of variation $Y$, gives $|\mathcal W_u(Y;L)|\ll Y\sum_{Y<d'\le u^2YL}\lambda\omega(d')\,\mathrm{disc}(\Delta^{(u)}_{d'})/d'\ll Y\log(u^2L)\,(\log Y)^{-c}$
by Proposition~\ref{prop:KSw} applied dyadically, and the factor $\log u$ is fatal: summed against $w(u)/u$ it
produces $H(\log H)^{2-c}$, which exceeds $H\log H$ since $c<1$. The averaging of Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} needs
$Y<u^{1/2}$. In the original variables the window consists of the moduli $d=ud'\in(H,u^2HL]$ with agreeing part
$u$, and the count is that of the roots $X\le H$ of $X^2\equiv D\pmod{d'}$ divisible by $u$: a short-interval
problem for the roots of a quadratic congruence, at the scale $Y/d'\ge(u^2L)^{-1}$ of the period.

\subsection{The window in Fourier form}

\begin{lemma}[finite Fourier expansion]\label{lem:finfourier}
Let $d'\ge3$ be odd, $R\subset\Z/d'$ symmetric ($x\in R\Rightarrow-x\in R$) with $0\notin R$, $Y\ge1$ an integer, and
$\rho_k=\sum_{x\in R}\e(kx/d')$ (real). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finfourier}
\sum_{x\in R}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\rep x{d'})=\frac1{4d'}\sum_{0<k<d'}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi kY/d')}{\sin^2(\pi k/d')}\,\rho_k
=\frac1{2d'}\sum_{0<k<d'/2}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi kY/d')}{\sin^2(\pi k/d')}\,\rho_k .
\end{equation}
For every $k$ the coefficient satisfies $0\le\frac{1-\cos(2\pi kY/d')}{4d'\sin^2(\pi k/d')}\le\frac1{d'}\min\Bigl(\frac{d'^2}{8k^2},\frac{\pi^2Y^2}{8}\Bigr)$ when $0<k\le d'/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $F(m)=B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)$, a function on $\Z/d'$, with Fourier coefficients $\hat F(k)=d'^{-1}\sum_{m}F(m)\e(-km/d')$, so
that $\sum_{x\in R}F(x)=\sum_{k\bmod d'}\hat F(k)\rho_k$. For $k\equiv0$, $\hat F(0)=d'^{-1}(\sum_{h\le Y}(Y-h)-Y^2/2)=-Y/(2d')$.
For $k\not\equiv0$ put $\alpha=k/d'$; since $\sum_m(Y^2/2d')\e(-km/d')=0$,
\[
\hat F(k)=\frac1{d'}\sum_{h\le Y}(Y-h)\e(-h\alpha)=\frac1{d'}\sum_{j=0}^{Y-1}\ \sum_{h=1}^{j}\e(-h\alpha)
=\frac1{d'}\,\frac{\e(-\alpha)}{1-\e(-\alpha)}\Bigl[Y-\frac{1-\e(-Y\alpha)}{1-\e(-\alpha)}\Bigr].
\]
Now $\e(-\alpha)/(1-\e(-\alpha))=1/(\e(\alpha)-1)$ has real part $-\tfrac12$, and
$\e(-\alpha)/(1-\e(-\alpha))^2\cdot(1-\e(-Y\alpha))=-(1-\e(-Y\alpha))/(4\sin^2\pi\alpha)$ has real part
$-(1-\cos2\pi Y\alpha)/(4\sin^2\pi\alpha)$. Hence $\Re\hat F(k)=-Y/(2d')+(1-\cos(2\pi kY/d'))/(4d'\sin^2(\pi k/d'))$.
Since $\rho_k$ is real and $\rho_{-k}=\rho_k$, $\sum_k\hat F(k)\rho_k=\sum_k\Re\hat F(k)\,\rho_k$, and
$\sum_{k\not\equiv0}\rho_k=\sum_{x\in R}\sum_{k\not\equiv0}\e(kx/d')=-|R|$ because $0\notin R$; so the terms $-Y/(2d')$
contribute $-|R|Y/(2d')+|R|Y/(2d')=0$, which is the first equality. The second is the symmetry $k\leftrightarrow d'-k$
($d'$ odd). For the bound, $1-\cos\phi\le\min(2,\phi^2/2)$ and $\sin(\pi k/d')\ge2k/d'$ for $0<k\le d'/2$.
\end{proof}

For $d'\le Y$, \eqref{eq:finfourier} is \eqref{eq:fourier} with the frequencies folded modulo $d'$. The
bound in the lemma shows what the window is made of: for $d'>Y$ the frequencies $k\le d'/Y$ carry the flat weight
$\asymp Y^2/d'$ each, and only beyond $k\asymp d'/Y$ does the $k^{-2}$ decay begin. A modulus $d'\in(Y,u^2YL]$
therefore has up to $u^2L$ frequencies of full weight, and any Fourier-side treatment must control the Weyl sums
$\rho_k(d')$ for $k$ up to $u^2L$, with a loss in $k$ small enough to be paid by the saving in the modulus. This is
the source of the exponent condition in Theorem~A.

\begin{hypothesis}[W$(\theta,B)$]\label{hyp:W}
There are $\theta\in(0,1)$ and $B\ge0$ such that for all squarefree $u$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$, all $k\ge1$, $t\ge2$ and
$\varepsilon>0$,
\[
S_k(t)=\sum_{\substack{d'\le t\\ (d',u)=1,\ d'\ \mathrm{split}}}\lambda(d')\sum_{x:\ u^2x^2\equiv D\ (d')}\e\Bigl(\frac{kx}{d'}\Bigr)\ll_{f,\varepsilon}(uk)^{B}\,t^{\theta+\varepsilon}.
\]
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{proposition}[the window from Weyl sums]\label{prop:windowW}
Assume Hypothesis~W$(\theta,B)$. Then for squarefree $u$ with $\omega(u)\ge1$, $Y\ge2$, $L\ge1$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
\mathcal W_u(Y;L)\ll_{f,\varepsilon}u^{3B+2\theta}L^{B+\theta}\,Y^{\theta+2\varepsilon}+Y^{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:finfourier} (all $d'$ are odd, $R^{(u)}_{d'}$ is symmetric and avoids $0$),
\[
\mathcal W_u(Y;L)=\frac12\sum_{k\ge1}\ \sum_{\substack{Y<d'\le u^2YL\\ d'>2k}}\lambda(d')\,\rho_k(d')\,g_k(d'),\qquad
g_k(d')=\frac{1-\cos(2\pi kY/d')}{d'^2\sin^2(\pi k/d')},
\]
the interchange being legitimate since all sums are finite. Let $K=u^2LY^{\varepsilon}$. For $k>K$ we use
$0\le g_k(d')\le1/(2k^2)$ (from $\sin(\pi k/d')\ge2k/d'$) and $|\rho_k|\le\omega(d')$: the contribution is
$\le\sum_{d'\le u^2YL}\lambda\omega(d')\sum_{k>K}k^{-2}\ll u^2YL/K=Y^{1-\varepsilon}$. For $k\le K$ we sum by parts in $d'$ over
the interval $I_k=(\max(Y,2k),u^2YL]$. On $I_k$ the factor $a(d')=1-\cos(2\pi kY/d')$ lies in $[0,2]$ and, since
$2\pi kY/d'$ decreases monotonically from at most $2\pi k$ to a positive value, has total variation $\le4k$; the
factor $b(d')=(d'\sin(\pi k/d'))^{-2}$ is decreasing (as $t\mapsto t\sin(\pi k/t)$ increases for $t>k$) and lies in
$(0,1/(4k^2)]$. Hence $\sup_{I_k}g_k\le1/(2k^2)$ and $\mathrm{Var}_{I_k}(g_k)\le2\cdot\frac1{4k^2}+\frac1{4k^2}\cdot4k\le\frac2k$.
Partial summation against $S_k$ (Hypothesis~W, with $\sup_{t\le u^2YL}|S_k(t)|\ll(uk)^B(u^2YL)^{\theta+\varepsilon}$) gives
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{d'\in I_k}\lambda\rho_k\,g_k\Bigr|\le2\sup_{t}|S_k(t)|\bigl(\sup g_k+\mathrm{Var}\,g_k\bigr)\ll(uk)^B(u^2YL)^{\theta+\varepsilon}\,k^{-1},
\]
and summing over $k\le K$: $\ll u^{B+2\theta+2\varepsilon}(YL)^{\theta+\varepsilon}K^{B}\log K\ll u^{3B+2\theta+O(\varepsilon)}L^{B+\theta+O(\varepsilon)}Y^{\theta+B\varepsilon+2\varepsilon}$.
Renaming $\varepsilon$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A]\label{thm:A}
Let $f$ be an irreducible quadratic and assume Hypothesis~W$(\theta,B)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expcond}
5\theta+6B<3\qquad\text{and}\qquad2B(2-\theta)<1-\theta .
\end{equation}
Then there is $\delta=\delta(\theta,B)>0$ with $\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)\,H+O_f(H^{1-\delta})$, and consequently
\[
\sum_{h\le H}\Bigl(1-\frac hH\Bigr)\bigl(S_f(h)-C(f)^2\bigr)=-\tfrac12C(f)\log H+A_f+o(1),\qquad
A_f=A^{\mathrm{diag}}_f+C(f)^2c_{\mathrm{off}}(f),
\]
Conjecture~1 of part~I in Ces\`aro form, where $A^{\mathrm{diag}}_f$ is the constant term of the diagonal
(part~I, Theorem~6; that the $O_f(1)$ there is $A^{\mathrm{diag}}_f+o(1)$ follows from the Perron integral of
$\zeta(s)D_f(s)H^{s+1}/(s(s+1))$ shifted past $\Re s=0$ using the zero-free region of $\zeta_K$, the double pole at
$s=0$ producing the $\log H$ and the constant; the rate of the $o(1)$ is governed by the zeros of $\zeta_K$ as in
part~II, Proposition~7).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varepsilon>0$ be small, $U_0=H^{2/3+\varepsilon}$, $L=H^{\varepsilon}$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} the pieces $u>U_0$ give
$c_{\mathrm{off}}H+O(H^{1-\delta_0})$. For $u\le U_0$ write $Y=H/u\ge H^{1/3-\varepsilon}$ and split
$\mathcal P_u(Y)=\mathcal P^\le_u(Y)+\mathcal W_u(Y;L)+\mathcal P^{\mathrm{far}}_u(Y;L)$.

\emph{Far moduli.} Lemma~\ref{lem:far}: $\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)\,H/(u^3L)\ll H^{1-\varepsilon}$.

\emph{Small moduli.} Hypothesis~W is~\eqref{eq:W}, so Lemma~\ref{lem:partial} gives
$T_k(Y)\ll(uk)^B(kY)^{1/(2-\theta)+\varepsilon}$, and in~\eqref{eq:Ple}
$\sum_{k\le K'}k^{-2}|T_k|\ll u^BY^{1/(2-\theta)+\varepsilon}\sum_{k\le K'}k^{-2+B+1/(2-\theta)+\varepsilon}\ll u^BY^{1/(2-\theta)+\varepsilon}$,
the exponent of $k$ being $<-1$ because $B+1/(2-\theta)<1$ follows from the second condition in~\eqref{eq:expcond}
(it is equivalent to $B(2-\theta)<1-\theta$); the tail $k>K'$ is $\ll Y/K'$ trivially, and $K'=Y$ suffices. Hence
$\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)\mathcal P^\le_u(H/u)\ll H^{1/(2-\theta)+\varepsilon}\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)u^{B-1/(2-\theta)}\ll H^{1/(2-\theta)+(2/3)(1+B-1/(2-\theta))+O(\varepsilon)}$,
and the exponent is $<1$ exactly when $2B(2-\theta)<1-\theta$.

\emph{Window.} Proposition~\ref{prop:windowW}:
$\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)\mathcal W_u(H/u;L)\ll H^{\theta+O(\varepsilon)}\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)u^{3B+\theta}+\sum_{u\le U_0}w(u)(H/u)^{1-\varepsilon}
\ll H^{\theta+(2/3)(1+3B+\theta)-1+O(\varepsilon)}\cdot H+H^{1-\varepsilon/3}$, and $\theta+\tfrac23(3B+\theta)<1$ is the first
condition in~\eqref{eq:expcond}. Choosing $\varepsilon$ small gives $\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}H+O(H^{1-\delta})$; the
consequence is~\eqref{eq:cesaro}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[what the exponents say]\label{rem:exponents}
The conditions~\eqref{eq:expcond} are satisfied by $(\theta,B)=(\tfrac12,B)$ with $B<\tfrac1{12}$, and by
$(\theta,0)$ with $\theta<\tfrac35$; they are not satisfied by the exponents $(\tfrac34,\tfrac14)$ of
\cite{DFI2012} for $u=1$, nor by any $\theta\ge\tfrac35$. So Hypothesis~W, to be useful here, must assert
square-root cancellation in the dilated Weyl sums on average over the modulus, with a loss in $u$ and $k$ below
$(uk)^{1/12}$; it is not a uniformity extension of the known theorems but a stronger statement, of the strength
one would obtain from the Kuznetsov formula only with square-root cancellation in the resulting Kloosterman-type
sums and no loss in the level. The bottleneck is the flat spectrum of the window: $u^2L$ frequencies of full
weight at every modulus, so that the saving $t^{1-\theta}$ in the modulus must pay for a factor $u^{3B+2\theta}$. The
range $2/3$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} enters through the factor $\tfrac23$ in the proof; an averaging argument
valid for $u>H^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ would relax the first condition to $3\theta+3B<2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the size of the pieces]\label{rem:piecesize}
The pieces can be computed exactly, and their size says how far the truth is from what is needed. For $f=t^2+1$ the
piece is $\mathcal P_u(Y)=\sum_{h\le Y}(Y-h)\bigl(F_u(Q'_u(h))-\mathbb EF_u\bigr)-(\mathbb EF_u-1)Y/2$ with
$Q'_u(h)=(u/2)^2h^2+1$, $F_u(n)=\prod_{p\mid n,\ p\equiv1(4),\ p\nmid u}(1+\tfrac1{p-4})$ and
$\mathbb EF_u=\prod_{p\equiv1(4),\ p\nmid u}(1+\tfrac2{p(p-4)})$ (the divisor-sum form of~\eqref{eq:piece}: $F_u(n)=\sum_{d'\mid n}\lambda(d')/d'$),
which is a sieve computation (\texttt{research/paper-IV/scripts/piece.ts}, with $\mathbb EF_u$ to $22$ digits). For $u\in\{2,10,26,130\}$
and $Y\le10^7$ (resp.\ $10^6$) we find $|\mathcal P_u(Y)|\le0.4\sqrt Y$ throughout, e.g.\ $\mathcal P_2(10^7)=42.7$ and
$\mathcal P_{10}(10^7)=229.8$, against the trivial size $Y\log(u^2L)$; and the sharp pieces
$S_u(t)=\sum_{h\le t}(F_u(Q'_u(h))-\mathbb EF_u)$, which are trivially $O(\log t)$, stay below $1.3$ in absolute value for all
$t\le10^7$. So the off-diagonal pieces exhibit square-root cancellation: each prime $p\mid Q'_u(h)$ contributes a sawtooth
of size $1/p$ in $t$, and the sawtooths at different moduli cancel like independent signs. This is the same phenomenon as
in the diagonal sum $\sum_da_f(d)(\{H/d\}-\tfrac12)$ of part~I, where the Dirichlet series proves it. What Theorem~\ref{thm:Aprime}
needs, $o(Y\log u)$ per piece on average, is very far from this truth; the gap between the two is the subject of part~IV.
\end{remark}

\emph{In words.} Two mechanisms are in place, and they meet with a gap between them. For pieces with a large
agreeing part the dilations average out along $u$, leaving the main term $c_{\mathrm{off}}H$; for small moduli in
every piece the Chinese remainder theorem alone equidistributes the dilated roots. Neither reaches the window: the
moduli just above the length, where an interval covers a fraction between $u^{-2}$ and $1$ of a period. Hypothesis~W
closes the window provided its exponents satisfy~\eqref{eq:expcond}, and the numerics for $t^2+1$ (the mean of the
sharp off-diagonal agrees with $c_{\mathrm{off}}+(1-D_f(1))/2$ to three digits) say that for that polynomial the
windows are indeed $o(H)$. We have not proved it, and we want to be clear that the window is not a technical
remainder: it is the off-diagonal problem itself, stated precisely, with everything else removed.

\section{The function field: the off-diagonal has a main term}\label{sec:ff}

Let $\mathcal A=\F_q[u]$, $q$ odd, $f=t^2-D$ with $D\in\mathcal A$ squarefree of positive degree, and define $\omega_f$,
$\lambda$, $P(1)$, $W_f$ as over $\Z$ with $|P|=q^{\deg P}$. Part~I, Theorem~8 and Lemma~9: for $N\ge1$, with the sum over
the $q^N-1$ nonzero $h$ of degree $<N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ffid}
\sum_h\Bigl(\frac{S_f(h)}{C^2}-1\Bigr)=-\sum_{1\le\deg d\le N}a_f(d)-q^N\sum_{\deg d>N}\frac{a_f(d)}{|d|}+\bigl(1-P(1)\bigr)+\Off_f(N),
\qquad\Off_f(N)=T_{\mathrm{act}}(N)-T_{\exp}(N),
\end{equation}
\[
T_{\mathrm{act}}(N)=\sum_{N<\deg d\le M(N)}W_f(d)A_N(d),\qquad T_{\exp}(N)=q^N\sum_{\deg d>N}\frac{W_f(d)\bigl(\omega_f(d)^2-\omega_f(d)\bigr)}{|d|},
\]
where $A_N(d)$ is the number of ordered pairs of distinct roots of $f$ modulo $d$ whose difference has degree $<N$
and $M(N)=N-1+\max(2N-2,\deg D)$. Here $\Off_f(N)$ is the exact off-diagonal (no Ces\`aro weight is needed: over
$\mathcal A$ the small moduli contribute nothing).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:B}
Let $D=u$. Then, as $q\to\infty$ through odd prime powers,
\[
\Off_f(1)=-\frac2q+O\Bigl(\frac1{q^2}\Bigr),\qquad\Off_f(2)=-\frac2q+O\Bigl(\frac1{q^{3/2}}\Bigr).
\]
More precisely $T_{\exp}(1)=2/q+O(q^{-2})$, $T_{\mathrm{act}}(1)=0$, $T_{\exp}(2)=3/q+O(q^{-3/2})$, $T_{\mathrm{act}}(2)=1/q+O(q^{-3/2})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Prime counts.} For $D=u$ the character $\chi_D$ has conductor of degree $1$, so its $L$-function is $1$ and
$\sum_{\deg P\mid k}\deg P\cdot\chi_D(P)^{k/\deg P}=0$ for every $k\ge1$. With $\pi_k$ the number of monic irreducible
polynomials of degree $k$ ($\pi_1=q$, $\pi_2=(q^2-q)/2$, $\pi_3=(q^3-q)/3$) this gives exactly: split linear primes
$(q-1)/2$ (the $a\in\F_q^\times$ with $\chi(a)=1$, $\chi$ the quadratic character of $\F_q$), one special prime $u$
($\omega=1$), inert linear primes $(q-1)/2$; split quadratic primes $(\pi_2-(q-1)/2)/2=(q-1)^2/4$; split cubic primes
$\pi_3/2$. For general $D$ the same counts hold up to $O(q^{k/2})$ by Weil's bound, which is why the error term
would be $O(q^{-3/2})$ in general; for $D=u$ and $N=1$ everything is exact and the error is $O(q^{-2})$.

\emph{Local factors.} At a split prime of norm $Q$, $\lambda=Q/(Q-4)=1+4/Q+O(Q^{-2})$ and $P_P=1-4/(Q-2)^2$; at $u$,
$\lambda=q/(q-2)$; at inert primes $\lambda=P_P=1$. Hence $P(1)=\prod_{\text{split linear}}(1-4/(q-2)^2)\cdot(1+O(q^{-2}))=1-2/q+O(q^{-2})$.

\emph{$T_{\exp}(1)$.} $T_{\exp}(1)=q\,P(1)\sum_{\deg d\ge2}\lambda(d)\omega^\circ(d)/|d|^2$ with $\omega^\circ=\omega^2-\omega$, over squarefree
$d$ with $\omega(d)\ge2$. Degree $2$: split quadratic primes, $\omega^\circ=2$, count $(q-1)^2/4$, weight $\lambda/q^4$; products of
two distinct split linear primes, $\omega=4$, $\omega^\circ=12$, count $\binom{(q-1)/2}2$; $u$ times a split linear prime,
$\omega^\circ=2$, count $(q-1)/2$. So $\sum_{\deg d=2}\lambda\omega^\circ=2\cdot\frac{q^2}4+12\cdot\frac{q^2}8+O(q)=2q^2+O(q)$, and
degree $\ge3$ contributes $q\cdot O(q^3\cdot q^{-6})=O(q^{-2})$. Hence $T_{\exp}(1)=q(1+O(q^{-1}))(2q^2+O(q))q^{-4}=2/q+O(q^{-2})$.
$T_{\mathrm{act}}(1)=0$ since $M(1)=1$ leaves no modulus.

\emph{$T_{\exp}(2)$.} Degree $3$ squarefree $d$ with $\omega(d)\ge2$: split cubic primes, $\omega^\circ=2$, count $\pi_3/2=q^3/6+O(q)$;
split linear $\times$ split quadratic, $\omega^\circ=12$, count $\frac{q-1}2\cdot\frac{(q-1)^2}4=q^3/8+O(q^2)$; three distinct split
linear, $\omega=8$, $\omega^\circ=56$, count $\binom{(q-1)/2}3=q^3/48+O(q^2)$; terms with $u$ are $O(q^2)$. So
$\sum_{\deg d=3}\lambda\omega^\circ=q^3\bigl(\tfrac26+\tfrac{12}8+\tfrac{56}{48}\bigr)+O(q^2)=3q^3+O(q^2)$, degree $\ge4$ gives
$q^2\cdot O(q^4q^{-8})=O(q^{-2})$, and $T_{\exp}(2)=q^2(1+O(q^{-1}))\cdot3q^3q^{-6}=3/q+O(q^{-2})$.

\emph{$T_{\mathrm{act}}(2)$.} $M(2)=3$, so the moduli have degree $3$. A pair $(s,s')$, $s'=-s$ on the differing part $d'$
and $s'=s$ on $d_2=d/d'$, has difference $m=s'-s$ with $d_2\mid m$ and $d'\mid m^2-4u$ (Lemma~9 of part~I:
$m\equiv-2s\pmod{d'}$, so $m^2\equiv4s^2\equiv4u$). For $\deg m\le1$: if $d_2=1$ then $\deg(m^2-4u)\le2<3=\deg d$
forces $m^2=4u$, impossible; if $\deg d_2=2$ then $d_2\mid m$ forces $m=0$, excluded; so $\deg d_2=1$, $m=cd_2$ with
$c\in\F_q^\times$, and $d'\mid c^2d_2^2-4u$ with $\deg d'=2=\deg(c^2d_2^2-4u)$, i.e.\ $d'=d_2^2-4u/c^2$ (monic). Conversely,
given a split or special linear $d_2=u-a$ ($\chi(a)=1$ or $a=0$) and $c\in\F_q^\times$, put $d'=(u-a)^2-4u/c^2$; then
$s_{d'}\equiv-cd_2/2$ is a square root of $u$ modulo $d'$ (as $(cd_2/2)^2\equiv u$), every prime of $d'$ is split, $d'$ is
coprime to $d_2$ (its value at $u=a$ is $-4a/c^2\ne0$ for $a\ne0$; for $a=0$, $d'=u^2-4u/c^2$ is divisible by $u$, so
$a=0$ is excluded, losing $O(q)$ of the $O(q^2)$ configurations), and $d'$ is squarefree unless its discriminant
$(16/c^2)(a+1/c^2)$ vanishes, i.e.\ unless $a=-1/c^2$ ($O(q)$ configurations). For each admissible $(d_2,c)$ and each
of the $\omega(d_2)=2$ choices of $s_{d_2}$ there is exactly one ordered pair with difference $cd_2$; the pairs with
difference $-cd_2$ are those of $(d_2,-c)$, which gives the same $d'$. Hence
\[
T_{\mathrm{act}}(2)=\sum_{(d_2,c)\ \mathrm{admissible}}2\,W_f(d_2d')=2P(1)\sum\frac{\lambda(d_2)\lambda(d')}{q^3}
=\frac{2}{q^3}\Bigl(\frac{q-1}2\Bigr)(q-1)\bigl(1+O(q^{-1})\bigr)=\frac1q+O\Bigl(\frac1{q^2}\Bigr).
\]
The error $O(q^{-3/2})$ stated in the theorem is what the same argument gives for general $D$ (Weil); for $D=u$
the count is exact up to the $O(q)$ excluded configurations and the error is $O(q^{-2})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the random permutation model]\label{rem:perm}
The leading term of $T_{\exp}(N)$ is $q^N\sum_{\deg d=N+1}\omega^\circ(d)q^{-2(N+1)}=q^{-1}\,\mathbb E_{N+1}[\omega^2-\omega]$, the
expectation over monic $d$ of degree $N+1$. As $q\to\infty$ the factorisation type of a random $d$ of degree $k$ is
distributed like the cycle type of a random permutation in $S_k$, and $\chi_D$ takes the values $\pm1$
independently and equiprobably on the prime factors (Weil), so that $\omega(d)=2^{\#\{P\mid d\}}$ if all factors are
split and $0$ otherwise, whence $\mathbb E_k[\omega]=1$ and $\mathbb E_k[\omega^2]=\mathbb E[2^{\mathrm{cycles}}]=(k+1)!/k!=k+1$. Thus
$T_{\exp}(N)\to(N+1)/q$ for every $N$ and every $D$, in agreement with $2/q$ and $3/q$ above, and the theorem says
$T_{\mathrm{act}}(N)\to(N-1)/q$ for $N=1,2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[configuration count]\label{rem:config}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} generalises to a heuristic for every $N$ and $D$. A pair of distinct roots
modulo $d$ with difference $m$ of degree $i\le N-1$ is a configuration $(d_2,\mu,d')$: $d_2\mid d$ monic of degree
$j\le i$ on which the roots agree, $m=d_2\mu$, $\deg\mu=i-j$, and $d'\mid m^2-4D$ of degree $k$, with $d=d_2d'$,
$\deg d=j+k\in(N,M(N)]$. Weighted by $W_f(d)\asymp q^{-(j+k)}$ and by the $\omega(d_2)$ choices of the root on $d_2$,
and using that $\sum_{\deg d_2=j}\omega(d_2)\sim q^j$, that there are $\sim q^{i-j+1}$ polynomials $\mu$, and that a
polynomial of degree $n$ has on average exactly one monic divisor of each degree $k\le n$ (for the family
$m^2-4D$ this is the equidistribution of the roots of $x^2\equiv4D$ among the residues of low degree, i.e.\ the
function-field Hypothesis~(E) itself), the configuration $(j,i,k)$ contributes $\asymp q^{\,i+1-j-k}$. The exponent
is $\le-1$ with equality exactly when $i=N-1$ and $k=N+1-j$ (moduli of degree $N+1$), subject to
$k\le\deg(m^2-4D)=\max(2N-2,\deg D)$. Hence
\[
T_{\mathrm{act}}(N)\;\to\;\frac{c_N(D)}q,\qquad c_N(D)=\#\{j\in[0,N-1]:\ N+1-j\le\max(2N-2,\deg D)\}=\min\bigl(N,\ \max(2N-2,\deg D)-1\bigr),
\]
while $T_{\exp}(N)\to(N+1)/q$ by Remark~\ref{rem:perm}. For $D=u$: $c_1=0$, $c_2=1$, in agreement with the theorem;
$c_N=N$ for $N\ge3$. The generic value $c_N=N$ leaves $\Off_f(N)\to-1/q$, and $1/q=q^N\sum_{\deg d=N+1}a_f(d)/|d|$ is
exactly the weight that the excluded diagonal pairs ($s=s'$, the ``configuration $j=N$'') would carry at degree
$N+1$: the off-diagonal cancels the first term of the diagonal tail.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:kappa}
For every squarefree $D$ of positive degree and every $N\ge1$,
\[
\Off_f(N)=-\frac{N+1-c_N(D)}q+O\Bigl(\frac1{q^{3/2}}\Bigr)\qquad(q\to\infty),
\]
with $c_N(D)$ as in Remark~\ref{rem:config}; in particular $\Off_f(N)=-1/q+O(q^{-3/2})$ for all $N\ge3$, and for
$N\le2$ unless $\deg D\le N$, where it is $-2/q+O(q^{-3/2})$.
\end{conjecture}

Table~\ref{tab:offq} and Figure~\ref{fig:offq} give the exact values for $D=u$, $u^2+1$, $u^3+u$ (\texttt{research/paper-III/scripts/offq.ts}).
They agree with the conjecture in every case: $q\,\Off_f(N)$ is near $-2$ for $(N,\deg D)=(1,1),(2,1),(2,2)$ and
near $-1$ for $(1,2),(1,3),(2,3)$; for $(3,1)$ with all moduli only $q\le7$ is available and the values $-4.9,-2.5,-2.0$
are still descending. Since the main term comes from the moduli of degree $N+1$ alone (the others contribute
$O(q^{-2})$), the leading constant can be tested at larger $q$ by restricting $T_{\mathrm{act}}$ to that degree: for
$D=u^2+1$, $N=3$ (degree $4$), $q\,T_{\mathrm{act}}$ is $2.5,2.4,2.9$ and $q\,\Off_f$ is $-1.06,-1.08,-1.20$ at $q=13,17,19$,

$q\,\Off_f=-1.6$ at $q=11$, against $c_4=4$, $5$ and $-1$ (with the same slow $1/q$ approach as at $N=1$). The fluctuations grow with $\deg D$, as they should:
the prime counts carry Weil terms $O(q^{k/2})$ from an $L$-function of degree $\deg D-1$, which is trivial for
$D=u$.

\emph{In words.} Over $\F_q[u]$ the off-diagonal is not just bounded: it has a main term of exact size $1/q$,
with a computable coefficient. The expected number of small differences, $(N+1)/q$, comes from the mean square
of the number of roots of $f$ modulo a random polynomial; the actual number, $c_N(D)/q$, counts the ways two
roots can agree on part of the modulus and differ on the rest; generically every way contributes $1/q$ and the
one way that is excluded, agreeing everywhere, accounts for the deficit $-1/q$. It is the first main term for
the off-diagonal in the programme, and it has the same origin as the constant $c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} over $\Z$: both measure the gap between the discrete count of root differences and its
continuous expectation, and both are negative. Over $\Z$ the gap sits at the moduli $d\asymp H$ (the pieces with
$u\asymp H$); over $\F_q[u]$ it sits at degree $N+1$.

\section{What remains}\label{sec:remains}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The window $\mathcal W_u(Y;L)$ for $1<u\le H^{2/3}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:Aprime}). Through Hypothesis~W this asks
for square-root cancellation in the Weyl sums of the dilated roots $u^2x^2\equiv D$ on average over the modulus,
with a loss below $(uk)^{1/12}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:exponents}); the route is Hooley's parametrisation with the
discriminant $4u^2D$, or the half-integral-weight Kuznetsov formula for $\Gamma_0(4u^2)$ as in \cite{DFI2012},
and in either case the level dependence is the whole question. A different route would avoid Weyl sums: an
averaging over $u$ in short ranges, combining the mechanisms of Theorems~\ref{thm:small} and~\ref{thm:typeII},
which would also extend the range $2/3$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:typeII} towards $1/2$ and relax the exponent
condition to $3\theta+3B<2$.
\item The constant: Theorem~\ref{thm:Aprime} identifies $c_{\mathrm{off}}$ only up to the logarithmic loss of the small
moduli. A power saving in Theorem~\ref{thm:small}, i.e.\ in Proposition~\ref{prop:KSw}, is not available (the
Kowalski--Soundararajan method saves a power of $\log$ only); so ``$\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}H+o(H)$''
unconditionally needs the small moduli of every piece to be treated by Weyl sums as well, which is again
Hypothesis~W.
\item A sharp-cut-off version of \cite{DFI2012} with $(\theta,B)=(\tfrac34,\tfrac14)$, which would improve the
exponent in Theorem~\ref{thm:u1}.
\item Conjecture~\ref{conj:kappa}, by the Lang--Weil count of the configurations $(d_2,\mu,d')$ of
Remark~\ref{rem:config}; the only input beyond counting is the equidistribution of the roots of $x^2\equiv4D$ modulo
$d'$ among the residues of degree $<\deg d'-1$, at the first sub-leading order, which is the function-field
Hypothesis~(E) at moduli of degree $N+1$. A proof for $N=3$, $D=u$ (moduli of degree $4$) would already decide
between the two constants.
\item The same programme for Galois groups that are not $2$-transitive, where the decomposition into pieces has
several orbit types and a saving over the trivial bound is not enough (part~I, Remark~7).
\end{enumerate}
