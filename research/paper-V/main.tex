\title{The pair singular series of a polynomial V:\\ the dilated pieces below $H^{1/2}$}
\author{Jasper Reichardt\thanks{Independent researcher. Correspondence: jasperreichardt@gmail.com.}}
\date{16 September 2026 --- working draft, not for circulation}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
For a monic irreducible quadratic $f$ without fixed prime divisor, part~III decomposed the Ces\`aro off-diagonal $\Off^\ast_f(H)$ into pieces indexed by the dilation $u$ and
proved that the pieces with $u>H^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ contribute a main term $c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H$. This paper treats dilations below that threshold. We prove that the pieces with
$H^{0.47}<u\le H^{0.53}$ contribute $O(H^{1-\delta})$, so that $\sum_{u>H^{0.47}}w(u)\mathcal P_u(H/u)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H+O(H^{1-\delta})$; the input is a bilinear estimate for
Kloosterman fractions of Bourgain and Garaev together with a bound for the additive energy of reciprocals, valid for every modulus, which we deduce from an argument of
Cilleruelo and Garaev. For negative discriminants we prove a second result at the other end: the pieces with $u\le H^{1/3-c}$ contribute $O(H^{1-\delta(c)})$, by running the
automorphic kernel method of Grimmelt and Merikoski with the leading coefficient $u^2$ tracked. What remains is a neighbourhood of $u=H^{1/3}$.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\paragraph{The problem.} Let $f=t^2+bt+c\in\Z[t]$ be monic, irreducible, without fixed prime divisor, of discriminant $D$. Part~III~\cite{ReichardtIII} wrote the Ces\`aro
off-diagonal of the pair singular series as a sum of \emph{pieces},
\[
\Off^\ast_f(H)=P(1)\sum_{u}\lambda(u)\omega(u)\,\mathcal P_u(H/u),
\]
one for each squarefree \emph{dilation} $u$, the piece $\mathcal P_u(Y)$ being the same problem at length $Y=H/u$ with the roots of $x^2\equiv D$ replaced by those of
$u^2x^2\equiv D$. Each piece is $O(Y)$, and the weights satisfy $\sum_{u\le H}|w(u)|/u\asymp\log H$: the trivial bound for the whole sum is $H\log H$, which is exactly the size
of the statement to be proved. Every piece must therefore be small, and the difficulty is uniformity in $u$.

\paragraph{Exponent coordinates.} Write $u=H^{\alpha}$. Each method available for the pieces gives a saving $H^{-s(\alpha)}$ over the trivial bound on an interval of $\alpha$,
and degenerates at the ends of that interval. In these coordinates part~III proves $s>0$ for $\alpha>2/3$ (averaging the dilations along $u$), and the present author's
extension of that theorem, recorded in the revised part~III, gives $s>0$ for $\alpha>1/2$ by a dispersion argument with Weil's bound. Below $1/2$ both mechanisms fail: the
incomplete Kloosterman sums that dispersion produces have modulus $[d_1,d_2]\asymp Y^2$, and Weil's bound is trivial once the range of $u$ is shorter than the square root of
that modulus. This paper supplies two further mechanisms, one on each side of $\alpha=1/3$.

\paragraph{Results.} Theorem~\ref{thm:M} treats $\alpha$ near $1/2$: the weight $w$ is decomposed as $1*\chi_D*\gamma$, and the resulting bilinear forms in $u$ are estimated by
Weil's bound when one factor is long (Type~I) or short (Type~II), and, in the remaining balanced case, by the bilinear bound of Bourgain and Garaev~\cite{BourgainGaraev2014}
for Kloosterman fractions. That bound needs an estimate for the additive energy of reciprocals; the one in~\cite{BourgainGaraev2014} is too weak at the sizes that occur here,
and Lemma~\ref{lem:E} supplies a stronger one, valid for every modulus, by transferring an argument of Cilleruelo and Garaev~\cite{CilleruelooGaraev2011} for points on modular
hyperbolas. Theorem~\ref{thm:S} treats small $\alpha$ for negative discriminants: piece $u$ is, after removing the content, a Type~I sum for the polynomial $u^2\ell^2+|D|$ in
the sense of Grimmelt and Merikoski~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}, and their automorphic kernel theorem~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025kernel} applies at level $u^2e$ once
the two kernel quantities are estimated with the dilation tracked. The diagonal is the number of orbits of the family, $\asymp u$; the off-diagonal, which their general bound
would give as $\asymp u^2$, is in fact bounded independently of $u$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Aoff}), because the distances inside the family are quantised in steps of $1/(4h)$ whatever
the dilation.

\paragraph{What remains.} Both mechanisms degenerate at $\alpha=1/3$, from opposite sides and for unrelated reasons: there the balanced Type~II atoms sit at $d^{1/4}$, where the
energy input is not strong enough, and the kernel method's diagonal $\asymp u$ meets the trivial bound. The final section explains what the residual band costs. Two points are
worth recording. A band of width $2\delta$ in $\alpha$ costs $\delta\cdot H(\log H)^{1-c}\log\log H$, not $\delta H\log H$, because part~III's Theorem~E applies piece by piece
inside it; so a failing method never makes the total worse. And if the savings on both sides vanish only \emph{at} $\alpha=1/3$, with implied constants that are powers of
$\log H$, then the blocks near the point contribute $O(H)$ in total, which would give $\Off^\ast_f(H)=O(H)$ --- Hypothesis~(E) of part~I~\cite{ReichardtI}, a bounded
second-order term in the Ces\`aro asymptotic. The constant $A_f$ needs strictly more: a saving at the point itself.

\section{Notation}
We follow part~III~\cite{ReichardtIII}. For a prime $p$ let $\omega(p)$ be the number of roots of $f$ modulo $p$, $\lambda(p)=p/(p-2\omega(p))$, $P_p=1-\omega(p)^2/(p-\omega(p))^2$,
$P(1)=\prod_pP_p$ and $w(u)=P(1)\lambda(u)\omega(u)$; $w$ is supported on squarefree $u$ and $\sum_{u\le t}|w(u)|\ll t$. A modulus $d'$ is \emph{admissible} if it is squarefree,
all its prime factors are split, and $(d',2Du)=1$; $R^{(u)}_{d'}=\{x\bmod d':u^2x^2\equiv D\}$, $\rho_k(d';a)=\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}\e(kar/d')$, and $\langle x\rangle_{d'}\in[1,d']$ is
the representative of $x$. With $B^{(Y)}_{d'}(m)=\sum_{h\le Y,\,h\equiv m\,(d')}(Y-h)-Y^2/(2d')$, the piece is
\[
\mathcal P_u(Y)=\sum_{d'>1\ \mathrm{admissible}}\frac{\lambda(d')}{d'}\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\langle x\rangle_{d'}),\qquad Y=H/u .
\]
We write $G_{d'}(a,Y)=\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\langle ar\rangle_{d'})$, $\bar G_{d'}(Y)$ for its mean over the units $a$, $G^\circ=G-\bar G$, and $\xi_k(d',N)=(1-\cos(2\pi
kN/d'))/\sin^2(\pi k/d')$ for the coefficients of the finite Fourier expansion of $B^{(N)}$ (part~III, Lemma~finfourier). Throughout, $x^\ast$ denotes the inverse of $x$ modulo the
modulus at hand, $e_m(z)=\e(z/m)$, and $\varepsilon>0$ is arbitrarily small. Parameters: $\delta=10^{-3}$, $\eta=\delta/10$, $\delta_3=\delta$, $\varepsilon'=\eta/7$,
$\kappa=\delta/2$, $K_1=H^{2\eta}$.
\section{The energy of reciprocals and bilinear Kloosterman fractions}

We write $x^\ast$ for the inverse of $x$ modulo the modulus at hand and $e_m(z)=\e(z/m)$.

\begin{lemma}[smooth incomplete Kloosterman sums]\label{lem:K}
Let $q\ge1$ be squarefree, $a\in\Z$, $X>0$ and $T\ge1$, and let $W$ be smooth, supported in an interval of length at most $4X$, with $\|W\|_\infty\ll1$ and $\int|W''|\ll T^2/X$. Then
\[
\sum_{(v,q)=1}W(v)\,e_q(av^\ast)\ \ll\ \tau(q)\Bigl(1+\frac Xq\Bigr)T^2q^{1/2}(a,q)^{1/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split $v$ into classes modulo $q$ and apply Poisson summation in each: the sum equals $\sum_{h}\widehat W(h/q)S(a,h;q)/q$ with $S(a,h;q)$ the Kloosterman sum. For squarefree $q$,
Weil's bound at the primes and twisted multiplicativity give $|S(a,h;q)|\le\tau(q)(a,h,q)^{1/2}q^{1/2}$, which covers $h=0$, where $S(a,0;q)=c_q(a)$ is a Ramanujan sum and
$|c_q(a)|\le(a,q)\le q^{1/2}(a,q)^{1/2}$. Since $\widehat W(\xi)\ll\min(X,T^2/(X\xi^2))$, one has $q^{-1}\sum_h|\widehat W(h/q)|\ll T^2(1+X/q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[additive energy of reciprocals, every squarefree modulus]\label{lem:E}
Let $m\ge2$ be squarefree, $1\le N\le m$, and
$J_4(N)=\#\{x\in[1,N]^4:(x_i,m)=1,\ x_1^\ast+x_2^\ast\equiv x_3^\ast+x_4^\ast\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)\}$. Then for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
J_4(N)\ \ll_\varepsilon\ m^{\varepsilon}\bigl(N^2+N^{7/2}m^{-1/2}\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: a hyperbola in a square box.} Let $(\lambda,m)=1$, $K\in\Z$ and $1\le M\le m$; we claim
$I:=\#\{(x,y)\in(K,K+M]^2:xy\equiv\lambda\ (m)\}\ll_\varepsilon m^{\varepsilon}(M^{3/2}m^{-1/2}+1)$. For $M=1$ this is trivial, so let $M\ge2$. Writing $x=K+x'$, $y=K+y'$ with
$1\le x',y'\le M$, the congruence becomes $x'y'+Kx'+Ky'\equiv b:=\lambda-K^2\ (m)$. Put $T=\lfloor(m/M)^{1/2}\rfloor\ge1$; by Dirichlet's theorem there are $1\le t\le T$ and $u_0$
with $tK\equiv u_0\ (m)$ and $|u_0|\le\min(m/T,m/2)$. Multiplying by $t$,
\[
tx'y'+u_0x'+u_0y'=b_0+zm,\qquad |b_0|<m/2,\quad |z|\le\frac{TM^2}m+\frac{2M}T+\frac12\le 5M^{3/2}m^{-1/2}+\tfrac12 .
\]
For each $z$ this is $(tx'+u_0)(ty'+u_0)=n_z:=t(b_0+zm)+u_0^2$, and $|n_z|\le7m^2$. Now $n_z\equiv t^2(x'+K)(y'+K)\equiv t^2\lambda\ (m)$, and $\lambda$ is a unit with
$1\le t^2\le m/M<m$, so $m\nmid n_z$ and in particular $n_z\ne0$. Each $z$ therefore contributes at most $\tau(|n_z|)\ll_\varepsilon m^{\varepsilon}$ pairs, and summing over the
$\ll M^{3/2}m^{-1/2}+1$ admissible $z$ gives the claim.

\emph{Step 2: from energy to hyperbolas.} Let $J_2(\lambda)=\#\{x,y\le N,\ (xy,m)=1:x^\ast+y^\ast\equiv\lambda\}$, so $J_4=\sum_\lambda J_2(\lambda)^2$ and
$\sum_\lambda J_2(\lambda)=N'^2\le N^2$ with $N'=\#\{x\le N:(x,m)=1\}$. For a unit $\lambda$ the congruence $x^\ast+y^\ast\equiv\lambda$ is equivalent to
$(x-\lambda^\ast)(y-\lambda^\ast)\equiv\lambda^{\ast2}$, a unit hyperbola in a square box with equal shifts, so Step~1 gives $J_2(\lambda)\ll m^{\varepsilon}(N^{3/2}m^{-1/2}+1)$.
If $g=\gcd(\lambda,m)>1$ then $p\mid g$ forces $x\equiv-y\ (p)$, hence $x\equiv-y\ (g)$ and $g\le2N$; modulo $m/g$ the residue $\lambda$ is a unit, so Step~1 applied there gives
$J_2(\lambda)\ll m^{\varepsilon}(g^{1/2}N^{3/2}m^{-1/2}+1)$ when $N\le m/g$, while $J_2(\lambda)\le N$ always. Using $\sum_{\gcd(\lambda,m)=g}J_2(\lambda)\le N(2N/g+1)$ and summing
over the divisors $g\le2N$ of $m$ yields the lemma; in the range $N>m/g$ one uses $4N^4/m\le4N^{7/2}m^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bourgain--Garaev]\label{lem:B}
Let $m\ge2$, $(a,m)=1$, $1\le N_1,N_2\le m$, and let $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ be coefficients bounded by $1$ and supported on units. Then for all $k_1,k_2\ge1$,
\[
\Bigl|\sum_{x_1\le N_1}\sum_{x_2\le N_2}\alpha_1(x_1)\alpha_2(x_2)e_m(ax_1^\ast x_2^\ast)\Bigr|^{2k_1k_2}\le m\,N_1^{2k_1k_2-2k_1}N_2^{2k_1k_2-2k_2}J_{2k_1}(N_1)J_{2k_2}(N_2).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the inequality established in the proof of Theorem~3 of~\cite{BourgainGaraev2014}: two applications of H\"older's inequality followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, using
$\sum_\lambda J_{k}(\lambda)\le N^{k}$ and that $a$ is a unit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:B}
For squarefree $m$ and $m^{1/3}\le N_1,N_2\le m$, under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:B},
$\bigl|\sum\sum\alpha_1\alpha_2e_m(ax_1^\ast x_2^\ast)\bigr|\ll_\varepsilon m^{\varepsilon}(N_1N_2)^{15/16}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $k_1=k_2=2$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:B} and insert Lemma~\ref{lem:E}: for $N_i\ge m^{1/3}$ the second term of the energy bound dominates, $N_i^{7/2}m^{-1/2}\ge N_i^2$, so the
right-hand side is $\ll m^{1+4\varepsilon}(N_1N_2)^4(N_1N_2)^{7/2}m^{-1}=m^{4\varepsilon}(N_1N_2)^{15/2}$.
\end{proof}

\section{The pieces around $u=H^{1/2}$}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:M}
Let $f$ be as above. There is $\delta_M>0$ (any $\delta_M<4\eta/7$ with the parameters above, so $\delta_M=5\cdot10^{-5}$ is admissible) such that
\[
\sum_{H^{0.47}<u\le H^{0.53}}w(u)\,\mathcal P_u(H/u)\ \ll_f\ H^{1-\delta_M}.
\]
Consequently, with part~III's Theorem on the range $u>H^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ applied at $\varepsilon=0.03$,
$\sum_{u>H^{0.47}}w(u)\mathcal P_u(H/u)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H+O(H^{1-\delta_M})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:M}] The proof occupies the rest of this section. It is organised as a reduction to a Mellin-separated bilinear form
(Step~R1), a decomposition of the weight $w$ with a covering of the resulting boxes (Step~R5), and three estimates: a Type~I bound (Step~R2), a Type~II bound (Step~R3) and the
residual balanced case (Step~R4), the last being where Corollary~\ref{cor:B} is used.
\paragraph{Notation for the proof.} Put $\delta=10^{-3}$, $\eta=\delta/10$, $\delta_3=\delta$, $\varepsilon'=\eta/7$, $\kappa=\delta/2$ and $K_1=H^{2\eta}$; all implied constants
depend on $f$ and on these parameters. For an admissible modulus $d'$ and a unit $a$ let $G_{d'}(a,Y)=\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}B^{(Y)}_{d'}(\langle ar\rangle_{d'})$, let $\bar G_{d'}(Y)$ be
its mean over the units and $G^\circ=G-\bar G$; let $\rho_k(d';a)=\sum_{r\in R_{d'}}\e(kar/d')$, $\epsilon_{d'}(k)=c_{d'}(k)/\varphi(d')$ and
$\xi_k(d',N)=(1-\cos(2\pi kN/d'))/\sin^2(\pi k/d')$, and set $b_k(d')=\min\bigl(1/(4k^2),\pi^2(2Y)^2/(4d'^2)\bigr)$.

\paragraph{Step R1: reduction to a Mellin-separated form.} Partition the range of $u$ smoothly into dyadic blocks, with ramps of relative width $H^{-\kappa}$ at the two endpoints;
the transition zones contain $\ll H^{0.53-\kappa}$ values of $u$ and contribute $\ll H^{1-\kappa}(\log H)^C$ by $\mathcal P_u(Y)\ll Y$. Fix a block, $U\in[H^{0.47}/4,2H^{0.53}]$,
$Y=H/U$, $a=\log U/\log H\in[0.46,0.54]$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] the dilation average contributes $\ll U(\log H)^C$, since $\sum_{d'}(\lambda(d')/d')|\bar G_{d'}(H/u)|\ll(\log Y)^C$ for every $u$;
\item[(b)] the moduli $d'>YH^{\eta}$ contribute $\ll H^{1-\eta+3\varepsilon'}$ by part~III's Step~3, which is valid for every $U$;
\item[(c)] the moduli $d'\le Y^{1-\delta}$ contribute $\ll UY^{1-\delta}$, by $|(\lambda/d')G^\circ|\le|\lambda|\omega(1+\sigma/(8d'\varphi))$ and Shiu's theorem~\cite{Shiu1980};
\item[(d)] on the remaining range the finite Fourier expansion of part~III applies at $N(u)=\lfloor H/u\rfloor$, the frequency $k=0$ cancelling exactly because $R_{d'}$ is symmetric
and $0\notin R_{d'}$; the frequencies $k>K_1$ contribute $\ll H^{1-\eta}$, and replacing $\xi_k(d',N(u))$ by $\xi_k(d',H/u)$ costs $\ll U(\log H)^C$. Note $d'\ge Y^{1-\delta}>2K_1$,
so $(k,d')\le K_1<d'$;
\item[(e)] with $F_{k,d'}(v)=\lambda(d')\xi_k(d',H/v)\phi(v/U)\psi(v)/(2d'^2)$ one has, for $T_0=1+kY/d'+H^{\kappa}$ and $J\in\{0,1,2\}$,
$\int|\widetilde F_{k,d'}(it)|(1+|t|)^J\,dt\ll|\lambda(d')|\,(Y/(kd'))\,T_0^J\log H$, by the total-variation bound for $|t|\le T_0$ and $J+2$ integrations by parts beyond it, using
that the ramp's derivatives are supported on a set of relative measure $\ll H^{-\kappa}$; summing, $\sum_{k\le K_1}\sum_{d'}|\lambda|\omega\int|\widetilde F_{k,d'}|\ll Y(\log H)^3$.
\end{enumerate}
The block therefore equals $(2\pi)^{-1}\int\sum_{k\le K_1}\sum_{d'}\widetilde F_{k,d'}(it)\,\mathcal S_{k,d'}(t)\,dt$ plus the errors above, where
$\mathcal S_{k,d'}(t)=\sum_uw(u)u^{-it}\mathbf 1_{(u,d')=1}\bigl[\rho_k(d';u^\ast)-\omega(d')\epsilon_{d'}(k)\bigr]$, and $u^{-it}$ factors over any factorisation of $u$. The mean
terms contribute $\ll UY^{\delta}(\log H)^C$.

\paragraph{Step R5: decomposition and covering.} Write $w=c_f(1*\chi_D*\gamma)$ with $\chi_D$ the primitive character of the field, $\gamma$ multiplicative with $\gamma(p)\ll1/p$,
$|\gamma(n)|\le20^{\omega(n)}$ and $\sum_n|\gamma(n)|n^{-1/2-\varepsilon}<\infty$ (for $\omega(2)=1$ put $u=2u'$ and absorb the Euler factor at $2$ into $\gamma$). The part with
$n_3>H^{\delta_3}$ is $\ll UYH^{-\delta_3/2+\varepsilon}$. Decompose $n_1,n_2,n_3$ into smooth dyadic boxes $N_1,N_2,N_3$, discarding boxes with $N_1N_2N_3\not\asymp U$, which
vanish. For $a\in[0.46,0.54]$ the boxes split into three cases: either some atom has $N_i\ge Y^{1/2}H^{3\delta}$ (Step~R2); or some atom lies in
$W=[H^{3\delta},UY^{-1/2}H^{-3\delta}]$ (Step~R3); or both $N_1,N_2\in[UY^{-1/2}H^{-3\delta},Y^{1/2}H^{3\delta}]$ (Step~R4). Indeed if $N_2<H^{3\delta}$ then
$N_1\gg UH^{-3\delta-\delta_3}\ge Y^{1/2}H^{3\delta}$, because $U/Y^{1/2}\ge H^{0.19}$ on the block; the covering is exhaustive with margin $0.183$.

\paragraph{Step R2 (Type I).} Let an atom of length $N\ge Y^{1/2}H^{3\delta}$ carry the weight $1$ (or $\chi_D$ restricted to a class modulo the conductor), the cofactor $m'$ carrying
arbitrary coefficients $\ll\tau_3(m')20^{\omega}$. For fixed $k,d',r,m',t$, with $g=(k,d')$ and $m=d'/g$, the atom sum is $\sum_nW(n)e_m\bigl((k/g)r(nm')^\ast\bigr)$ with
$W(n)=\phi(n/N)n^{-it}$ after removing $(n,g)=1$ by M\"obius, so Lemma~\ref{lem:K} with $T=1+|t|$ applies and gives $\ll H^{\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^2(1+N/m)m^{1/2}$. Summing over
$m'\ll U/N$, the roots, the moduli against $\int|\widetilde F|(1+|t|)^2$ and $k\le K_1$, and splitting the two branches of $T_0^2\ll1+(kY/d')^2+H^{2\kappa}$, the contribution is
$\ll UY\bigl(H^{-3\delta+4\eta+1.5(1-a)\delta}+H^{-3\delta+\eta/2+2\kappa}\bigr)+UY^{1/2+O(\delta)}\ll UY\,H^{-1.79\delta}$.

\paragraph{Step R3 (Type II).} Let $u=u_1u_2$ with $|a(u_1)|,|b(u_2)|\ll H^{\varepsilon}$ and $U_2\in W$. Apply Cauchy--Schwarz over $(d',u_1)$ with weight $|\beta(d')|$,
$\beta=\widetilde F_{k,d'}(it)$, replacing the $u_1$-range by a smooth majorant. The diagonal gives $S_1\ll H^{2\varepsilon}U_1\sum_{d'}|\beta|$. In $S_2$ the terms carrying
$\epsilon_{d'}$ are negligible, and the main $u_1$-sums are $\sum V(u_1/U_1)e_{d'}(\nu u_1^\ast)$ with $\nu\equiv k(ru_2^\ast-r'u_2'^\ast)$; Lemma~\ref{lem:K} bounds each by
$\tau(d')(1+U_1/d')d'^{1/2}(\nu,d')^{1/2}$, and trivially by $3U_1$. Writing $(\nu,d')=(k,d')e$ with $e=\prod_{p\mid d',\,p\nmid k,\,ru_2'\equiv r'u_2\,(p)}p$, for fixed
$(d',r,r',u_2)$ there are at most $4U_2/e$ values of $u_2'$ with that $e$ when $e\le2U_2$, and at most one when $e>2U_2$ --- the latter case containing the diagonal and all degenerate
pairs, since $d'/(k,d')>2U_2$. Hence $S_2\ll H^{O(\varepsilon+\eta)}\sum_{d'}|\beta|\bigl[U_2^2Y^{1/2}+U_1U_2^2Y^{-1/2}+U_1U_2\bigr]$, the middle term alone carrying the loss
$Y^{\delta/2}$, and therefore the contribution is $\ll UY\,H^{-1.45\delta}$.

\paragraph{Step R4 (the residual).} Here both atoms lie in $[UY^{-1/2}H^{-3\delta},Y^{1/2}H^{3\delta}]$. For fixed $k,d',r,n_3,t$, with $g=(k,d')$ and $m=d'/g$ squarefree,
$m\ge Y^{1-\delta}H^{-2\eta}$, the numerator $(k/g)rn_3^\ast$ is a unit modulo $m$ and the double sum is of the shape treated in Corollary~\ref{cor:B}, with the coprimality
conditions and the dyadic cut-offs absorbed into the coefficients. On the block, $\log N_i/\log m\ge0.3462>1/3$ and $2N_i\le m$, so Corollary~\ref{cor:B} applies and gives
$\ll m^{\varepsilon}(N_1N_2)^{15/16}$. Since $N_1N_2\asymp U/n_3$, summing over $n_3$ against $|\gamma|$, over the roots, over the moduli against $\int|\widetilde F|$ and over
$k\le K_1$ gives $\ll UY\,H^{-0.028}$.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Per block the errors are $H^{1-4\eta/7}$ from R1(b), $H^{1-\eta}$ from R1(d), $UY^{1-\delta}$ from R1(c), $UY^{\delta}$ from the mean terms,
$H^{1-\delta_3/2+\varepsilon}$ from the $n_3$-tail, $H^{1-\kappa}(\log H)^C$ from the ramps, and the three savings above. With $O(\log H)$ blocks the binding constraint is R1(b), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:M} follows for every $\delta_M<4\eta/7$.
\end{proof}

\section{The small dilations}

Throughout this section $D<0$. We write $h$ for a positive integer, $\Gamma_0(q)$ for the Hecke congruence subgroup, $\Gamma_0^0(u)=\{\gamma:u\mid b,\ u\mid c\}$, and for
positive definite forms $g_i=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A_i&B_i\\B_i&C_i\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ we put $\langle g_1,g_2\rangle=A_1C_2+A_2C_1-2B_1B_2$, so that the point-pair
invariant of the associated points of the upper half plane is $u(g_1,g_2)=(\cosh d-1)/2$ with $\cosh d=\langle g_1,g_2\rangle/(2\sqrt{\det g_1\det g_2})$. The kernel method
of Grimmelt and Merikoski is used in the form of their Theorem~8.1~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025kernel} and Lemma~3.1~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}; we follow their
notation, in particular $\Delta_q$, $K_q$, the kernel quantities $\mathcal K_1(q;Z,R)$, $\mathcal K_2(q;Z)$ and the functional $\alpha_q$ on the Heegner set.

\subsection{The family of a dilated piece, and its normal form}\label{sec:normal}
The piece $\mathcal P_u(Y)$ counts the roots of $Q_u(\ell)=u^2\ell^2+|D|$ modulo admissible $k$, which are coprime to $2Du$. The content of $Q_u$ is $c=\gcd(u^2,|D|)$, and since
$u$ is squarefree, $v_p(c)=\min(2,v_p(D))$ for $p\mid u$, so $c=Ps^2$ with
\[
P=\prod_{p\mid u,\ v_p(D)=1}p,\qquad s=\prod_{p\mid u,\ v_p(D)\ge2}p,\qquad u'=u/(Ps),\qquad a=Pu'^2,\qquad h=\frac{|D|}{Ps^2},\qquad h_0=Ph=\frac{|D|}{s^2}.
\]
Then $\gcd(a,h)=1$, $P\parallel h_0$, $h_0=O_D(1)$, and $(u',h_0)=1$; moreover $u'$ is odd: either $D$ is odd and $w$ is supported on odd $u$ (a root of $f$ modulo $2$ would
be a fixed prime divisor), or $D$ is even, hence $4\mid D$ (discriminants are $\equiv0,1\bmod4$), and the prime $2$ enters $s$. Since the primes of $c$ divide $u$ and $k$ is
coprime to $u$, $k\mid Q_u(\ell)$ if and only if $k\mid a\ell^2+h$: the piece is a Type~I sum for $a\ell^2+h$ in the sense of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}, and by
Lemma~3.1 there the pairs $(k,\ell)$ with $k\equiv0\ (q_0)$ correspond to the family
\[
\mathcal S_{a,h}(q_0)=\bigl\{g=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\B&C\end{smallmatrix}\bigr):\ AC-B^2=ah,\ A,C>0,\ a\mid B,\ aq_0\mid C\bigr\}
\]
at level $q=aq_0$, the functional being $\alpha_q(g)=\mathbf 1_{q\mid c(g)}\mathbf 1_{a\mid b(g)}$ on the Heegner set $\Lambda_{ah}$ (their $b^2-ac=ah$ is a slip for $ac-b^2=ah$).
The case $P=s=1$ is $a=u^2$, the case treated in our earlier drafts; the general normal form costs nothing extra, as Lemma~\ref{lem:Aoff} shows.

\subsection{The off-diagonal at one level does not grow with the dilation}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Aoff}
Let $(a,h)=(Pu'^2,|D|/(Ps^2))$ be as in \S\ref{sec:normal}, $F=\mathcal S_{a,h}(1)$, and for $Z\ge1$
\[
\mathrm{off}(a;Z)=\sum_{g_2\in\Gamma_0(a)\backslash F}\ \sum_{\substack{g_1\in F,\ g_1\ne g_2\\ u(g_1,g_2)\le Z}}\bigl(1+u(g_1,g_2)\bigr)^{-1/2},
\]
the orbits counted with the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$-stabiliser weights $|\Gamma_g|^{-1}\le1$. Then $\mathrm{off}(a;Z)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}(Zu)^{\varepsilon}(1+Z^{1/2})$, uniformly in
$u$: the $u$-dependence is the divisor factor $12^{\omega(u')}$, absorbed by $(Zu)^\varepsilon$, and there is no power of $h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(1) Undilation.} Let $\delta=\mathrm{diag}(1,1/u')$ and $G=\delta g\delta$. For $g\in F$ write $B=au'^2B_1$... more precisely $B=aB_1$, $C=aC_1$; then
$G=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}A&Pu'B_1\\Pu'B_1&PC_1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has $\det G=ah/u'^2=h_0$, and $g\mapsto G$ is a bijection of $F$ onto
$F'=\{G:\det G=h_0,\ Pu'\mid B_G,\ P\mid C_G\}$, with $\delta\Gamma_0(a)\delta^{-1}=\{\gamma:u'\mid b,\ Pu'\mid c\}=\Gamma_0(P)\cap\Gamma_0^0(u')$ (using $(P,u')=1$). The map is
an isometry of the associated points, and $\langle g_1,g_2\rangle=u'^2\langle G_1,G_2\rangle=:u'^2N$ with $1+2u(g_1,g_2)=N/(2h_0)$. Hence $N\in\Z$, $N>2h_0$ for $g_1\ne g_2$, and
\[
\mathrm{off}(a;Z)=\sum_{2h_0<N\le2h_0(1+2Z)}\Bigl(\frac{4h_0}{N+2h_0}\Bigr)^{1/2}P_u(N),\qquad P_u(N)=\#\bigl\{(\Gamma_0(P)\cap\Gamma_0^0(u'))\text{-orbits of pairs }(G_1,G_2)\in F'^2,\ \langle G_1,G_2\rangle=N\bigr\}.
\]
\emph{(2) Lift to level one.} The stabiliser in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$ of a pair of distinct points is $\pm I$, so each orbit lies in a unique $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$-orbit of pairs
of determinant-$h_0$ forms with $\langle G_1,G_2\rangle=N$, and $P_u(N)=\sum_{\mathcal O}\#\{\tau\in(\Gamma_0(P)\cap\Gamma_0^0(u'))\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\tau.G_1,\tau.G_2\in F'\}$
over the $P_1(N)$ such orbits, evaluated at any representative. Taking $G_2$ among the reduced forms of determinant $h_0$ and observing that $W=2h_0G_1-NG_2$ lies in the
rank-two lattice $G_2^\perp$ with $-\det W=h_0(N^2-4h_0^2)$, the number of representations of a fixed integer by a definite binary lattice gives $P_1(N)\ll_D N^{o(1)}$ ---
here $h_0=O_D(1)$ is what makes the bound uniform.
\emph{(3) The local count.} By the Chinese remainder theorem the coset space factors over the primes of $Pu'$; parametrise $T_p\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\F_p)$ by the ordered pair of
distinct lines spanned by the rows of $\tau$, and $\Gamma_0(p)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\F_p)$ by the second row $L_2\in\mathbb P^1(\F_p)$. Then $p\mid B(\tau.G)$ reads $L_1\perp_GL_2$
and $p\mid C(\tau.G)$ reads $Q_G(L_2)=0$. For $p\mid u'$ (so $p\nmid2h_0$) the condition is $u'\mid B$ only: for one form there are $p-\chi_{-h_0}(p)$ cosets; for a pair, $L_2$ must
be an eigenline of $G_2^{-1}G_1$, whose characteristic polynomial is $\lambda^2-(N/h_0)\lambda+1$, so there are at most $2$ cosets unless $G_2^{-1}G_1$ is scalar modulo $p$, in which
case $p^2\mid N^2-4h_0^2$ and there are at most $p+1$. For $p\mid P$ both conditions are imposed, and they collapse to one: $p\mid B_G$, $p\mid C_G$ and $p\parallel h_0=\det G$
force $p\nmid A_G$, so $G\equiv\mathrm{diag}(A_G,0)$ is of rank one modulo $p$, its unique projective zero is its kernel line $L_0$, and for $L_2=L_0$ one has $GL_2\equiv0$, so
$L_1\perp_GL_2$ for every $L_1$. Hence the local factor at $p\mid P$ is $1$, for one form and for pairs, and altogether the number of cosets for a pair is at most $2^{\omega(u')}g_2(N)$
with $g_2(N)=\prod_{p\mid u',\,p^2\mid N^2-4h_0^2}p$.
\emph{(4) The sum over $N$.} With $M=2h_0(1+2Z)$ and $d$ running over the divisors of $u'$ with $d\le M$ (which is automatic, since $d^2\mid N^2-4h_0^2<M^2$),
\[
\sum_{2h_0<N\le M}N^{-1/2}g_2(N)\ \le\ \sum_{d\mid u',\,d\le M}d\sum_{\substack{N\le M\\ d^2\mid N^2-4h_0^2}}N^{-1/2}\ \le\ \sum_{d\mid u',\,d\le M}d\cdot2^{\omega(d)}\bigl(d^{-1/2}+2M^{1/2}/d^2\bigr)\ \le\ 3\tau_3(u')M^{1/2},
\]
because the $2^{\omega(d)}$ classes $N\bmod d^2$ with $d^2\mid N^2-4h_0^2$ each have $N\ge d$. Combining (1)--(4), $\mathrm{off}(a;Z)\ll_{D,\varepsilon}N^{o(1)}2^{\omega(u')}\tau_3(u')M^{1/2}\ll(Zu)^{\varepsilon}(1+Z^{1/2})$.
\end{proof}
The mechanism is that the distances between family points are quantised: $\cosh d\in(2h_0)^{-1}\Z$ independently of the dilation, so the number of pairs at distance $\le Z$ is
governed by the divisors of $N^2-4h_0^2$, not by the size of the level. Numerically, for $a=3u'^2$ and $a=15u'^2$ ($D=-3$, $-15$) the diagonal is exactly
$c\prod_{p\mid u'}(p-\chi_{-h_0}(p))$ with the factor at the primes of $P$ equal to $1$, and $\mathrm{off}(a;Z)/Z^{1/2}$ shows no growth in $u'$; the local count of step~(3) was also
checked by enumerating $\mathrm{SL}_2(\F_p)$ for $141$ normal forms, nine of them with $D$ non-fundamental.

\subsection{Theorem S}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:S}
Let $f$ be as above with $D<0$. For every $c>0$,
\[\sum_{\substack{u\le H^{1/3-c}\\ (u,D)=1}}w(u)\mathcal P_u(H/u)\ \ll_{f,c}\ H^{1-\delta_S(c)},\qquad \delta_S(c)=\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac19\cdot\tfrac32c(1-2\theta)\approx\frac{c}{12},\]
where $\theta=7/64$ is the Kim--Sarnak bound. The parameters below may be taken as $\delta=\eta=\eta'=\frac19\cdot\frac32c(1-2\theta)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}[on the coprimality restriction]\label{rem:uD}
Every step of the proof is written for the general normal form of \S\ref{sec:normal}; the only place where $(u,D)=1$ is still used is the level sum of \S\ref{sec:S6}, whose
restatement at $(u',h_0)$ is recorded in the repository and awaits an independent reading. Once that is in, the restriction disappears. The prime $2$ needs no hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Step S1: the reduction}
Fix $u$ squarefree with $u\le H^{1/3-c}$ and $Y=H/u$. Since $R^{(u)}_k$ is symmetric and $0\notin R^{(u)}_k$ (as $k$ is coprime to $2D$), pairing $x\leftrightarrow k-x$ in the
definition of the piece gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S1}
\sum_{x\in R^{(u)}_k}B^{(Y)}_k(\langle x\rangle_k)=\frac12\sum_{\ell\ne0,\ k\mid a\ell^2+h}T(\ell)-\frac{\varrho(k)}{2k}\int_\R T,\qquad T(t)=(Y-|t|)^+,\quad\varrho(k)=|R^{(u)}_k|,
\end{equation}
the shape ``count minus density times mass'' of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}. The small moduli $k\le Y^{1-\delta}$ contribute $\ll\sum_{k\le Y^{1-\delta}}|\lambda(k)|\varrho(k)\ll Y^{1-\delta}(\log Y)^{O(1)}$,
since for each pair $\{x,k-x\}$ the bracket $B^{(Y)}_k(m)+B^{(Y)}_k(k-m)$ lies in $[-k/4,k/4]$ (part~I, proof of Theorem~5) and Shiu's theorem bounds the sum; no smoothing is needed there,
the identity being exact for each $k$. The large moduli $k>Y^{1+\eta}$ contribute $\ll Y^{1-\eta}(\log Y)^{11}$ by part~III's Lemma~tail, uniformly in $u\le Y^3$. To hand a smooth
weight to the kernel theorem we insert $\psi_1$, supported on $[Y^{1-\delta}/2,2Y^{1+\eta}]$ and equal to $1$ on $[Y^{1-\delta},Y^{1+\eta}]$, and remove the two transition ranges by the
same two bounds. On the window we write, in this order,
\[
\lambda(k)\mu^2(k)\mathbf 1_{(k,2D)=1}=\sum_{j\mid(k,\mathrm{rad}\,2D)}\mu(j)\sum_{e\mid k}\kappa(e)\sum_{m^2\mid k}\mu(m),
\]
with $\kappa$ supported on split squarefree integers (so that $1*\kappa$ reproduces $\lambda$ only after the $j$-M\"obius) and $(k,u)=1$ automatic (a prime of $(k,u)$ would divide
$|D|$, hence $2D$); the tail $m>M:=Y^{\eta}$ is removed \emph{before} any further decomposition at a cost $\ll Y^{1-\eta+\varepsilon}$, and no truncation in $e$ is needed. Then $T$ is
decomposed into smooth dyadic boxes $T_L(\ell)=Y\psi_2(\ell/L)$ in $|\ell|\sim L$, the two kinks of $T$ at $\ell=\pm Y$ smoothed at scale $\Delta=Y^{1-\eta'}$, which changes the sum by
$\ll Y^{1+\delta-2\eta'+\varepsilon}$ in total and gives $\psi_2^{(J)}\ll Y^{J\eta'}$. A box $(L,K)$, $K$ dyadic in $[Y^{1-\delta},Y^{1+\eta}]$, has trivial size $\ll(Y/K)LH^{\varepsilon}$, so
the boxes with $L\le Y^{1-3\delta}$ contribute $\ll Y^{1-2\delta+\varepsilon}$ together and the kink of $T$ at $\ell=0$ never matters. The outcome: for every $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S1out}
\mathcal P_u(Y)=\sum_{\text{boxes }(L,K)}\ \sum_{j,e,m\le M}\mu(j)\kappa(e)\mu(m)\,\mathfrak B(L,K;q_0)+O\bigl(Y^{1-\min(\delta,\eta,2\eta'-\delta)+\varepsilon}\bigr),\qquad q_0=\mathrm{lcm}(j,e,m^2),
\end{equation}
with $O(\log^2H)$ boxes, $Y^{1-3\delta}\le L\le Y$, and
$\mathfrak B(L,K;q_0)=\frac12\sum_{k\equiv0\,(q_0)}\frac1k\psi_1(k/K)\bigl[\sum_{k\mid a\ell^2+h}T_L(\ell)-\varrho(k)\int T_L/k\bigr]$; the levels are $q=aq_0$ with
$\gcd(a,q_0)=1$ (for $p\mid P$, $k\mid a\ell^2+h\equiv h\ (p)$ and $p\nmid h$ force $p\nmid k$; for $p\mid u'$, $p\nmid2D$ and $(k,u)=1$).

\subsubsection*{Step S2: the two-scale form}
Following \S5 of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}, replace $T_L$ by a second weight $T^{(2)}(\ell)=(\int T_L)\,\psi(\ell/X_2)/(X_2\int\psi)$ of the same mass at the scale
$X_2=K^{1+\eta}>L$ (i.e.\ $\eta(1-\delta)>\delta$, which the parameters satisfy). The density terms of the two scales are identical, so
$\mathfrak B(L,K;q_0)=\mathcal A_1-\mathcal A_2+\mathcal E$, where $\mathcal A_i=\langle I|\Delta_qF_i|\alpha_q\rangle$ is built from $(\psi_1,T_L)$, resp.\ $(\psi_1,T^{(2)})$, exactly as in
their \S5, and $\mathcal E$ is the second-scale sum $\sum_{k\equiv0(q_0)}\frac1k\psi_1(k/K)[\sum_{k\mid a\ell^2+h}T^{(2)}(\ell)-\varrho(k)\int T^{(2)}/k]$, which Poisson summation modulo $k$
evaluates: for $k\sim K$ the range $X_2$ exceeds $k$ by the factor $K^{\eta}$, the dual frequencies are $\ge K^{\eta}$, and $\mathcal E\ll_A K^{-A}$ for every $A$, uniformly in $u$
(the constants depend on $A,\eta,\psi$). The skew of $F_1$ is $X/Y=Lu|D|^{-1/2}$ with $x$-range $R=L/K\in[Y^{-\eta-3\delta},Y^{\delta}]$; that of $F_2$ is larger by $X_2/L$, and its bound
is smaller than that of $\mathcal A_1$ by $(L/X_2)^{1/2-\theta}$, so only $\mathcal A_1$ is estimated below.

\subsubsection*{Step S3: the hypotheses of the kernel theorem}
In Iwasawa coordinates $x=\ell/k$, $y=\sqrt h/(uk)$ the weight of $\mathcal A_1$ is $F(g)=(Y/K)f(x,y)$ with $f(x,y)=\psi_2(\ell/L)\tilde\psi_1(k/K)$, $\tilde\psi_1(t)=\psi_1(t)/t$;
$f$ is supported on $|x|\asymp L/K$, $y\asymp\sqrt h/(uK)$, is not a product (the argument of $\psi_2$ depends on $y$ too), and each $\partial_y$ produces a factor $1/y$, so
$\partial_x^{J_1}\partial_y^{J_2}f\ll(Y^{\eta'})^{J_1+J_2}(L/K)^{-J_1}(\sqrt h/(uK))^{-J_2}$, the only non-dyadic scale being the kink smoothing. After splitting the $x$-support (of ratio
$\le4$) into $O(1)$ smooth dyadic pieces, $f\in C^{10}_{\delta_{\mathrm{GM}}}(X,Y)$ in the sense of Definition~2 of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025kernel} with $\delta_{\mathrm{GM}}^{-1}\asymp Y^{\eta'}$,
and $X/Y=Lu|D|^{-1/2}>\delta_{\mathrm{GM}}$ on every box. The Hecke variable is trivial, so their Rankin--Selberg input is not invoked; $q^{o(1)}=(u^2q_0)^{o(1)}=H^{o(1)}$; and the
unspecified $\delta_{\mathrm{GM}}^{-O(1)}$ of Theorem~8.1 is $\delta_{\mathrm{GM}}^{-C}$ with $C=6$ by Remark~\ref{rem:CGM}. Theorem~8.1 then gives, for every $Z_0,Z_1,Z_2\ge1$ with
$Z_0Z_1Z_2\ge X/Y+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GM}
|\mathcal A_1|\ \ll\ H^{o(1)}Y^{C\eta'}\,\frac YK\,(Lu)^{1/2}Z_0^{\theta}\,\mathcal K_1(q;Z_1,R)^{1/2}\mathcal K_2(q;Z_2)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Step S4: the first kernel quantity}
The example in \S1.2 of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025kernel} gives, for $q\ge1$, $Z\ge1$ and $0<R\le H^{O(1)}$, $\mathcal K_1(q;Z,R)\ll H^{o(1)}(1+R+1/(qR)+Z/q)$ (the cases
$c=0$, $b=0$, $bc\ne0$ of $\gamma\in\Gamma_0(q)$; we checked it numerically at our levels). Summed over the levels $q=a\,\mathrm{lcm}(j,e,m^2)$ of a block $e\sim E$, $m\sim M$, with
$1/R\le Y^{\eta+3\delta}$ and $R\le Y^{\delta}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K1}
\sum_{\text{levels}}\mathcal K_1\ \ll_D\ H^{o(1)}Y^{3\delta+\eta}\bigl(EM+Z_1/a\bigr).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Step S5: the second kernel quantity, the dictionary and the diagonal}
By definition (\S4.1 of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic}) and since $\alpha_q\ge0$ and the subtracted volume term is $\ge0$,
$\langle\alpha_q|\Delta_qk|\alpha_q\rangle\le\langle\alpha_q|K_qk|\alpha_q\rangle$, and the smooth kernel may be replaced by the majorant $\mathbf 1_{u\le Z_2^2}(1+u)^{-1/2}$. Unfolding
the $\gamma$-sum once gives the orbit form $\mathrm{diag}(q)+\mathrm{off}(q;Z_2)$ with the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$-stabiliser weights on the $g_2$-side and the $g_1$-side unweighted
(with $\Gamma_0(q)$-stabilisers the identity fails by a factor $2$). Unfolding a second time, as in Proposition~4.1 of~\cite{GrimmeltMerikoski2025quadratic},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge}
\mathrm{off}(aq_0;Z_2)=\sum_{z_2\in\Lambda_{ah}}|\Gamma_{z_2}|^{-1}\sum_{\substack{w_1\in\mathcal S_{ah}\\ w_1\ne z_2}}k\bigl(u(w_1,z_2)\bigr)\,n_{aq_0}(w_1,z_2),\qquad
n_q(w_1,z_2)=\#\{\tau\in\Gamma_0(q)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\alpha_q(\tau\diamond w_1)=\alpha_q(\tau\diamond z_2)=1\},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal S_{ah}$ the set of all forms of determinant $ah$, and for $\gcd(a,q_0)=1$ the Chinese remainder theorem gives $n_{aq_0}=n_a\,n_{q_0}$ with
$n_{q_0}=\#\{(c_0:d_0)\in\mathbb P^1(\Z/q_0):q_0\mid Q_{w_1}(c_0,d_0),\ q_0\mid Q_{z_2}(c_0,d_0)\}$. Summing $n_a$ over the $\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)$-orbits of pairs with invariant $N$ is the
count $P_u(N)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:Aoff}: the $a$-part of the off-diagonal is that lemma, and the $q_0$-part is the level sum below. The diagonal is the weighted number of
$\Gamma_0(q)$-orbits of the family, $\mathrm{diag}(q)=c_d(u)\rho^\ast(q_0)$ with $c_d(u)\asymp u$ exactly $\prod_{p\mid u'}(p-\chi_{-h_0}(p))$ times a factor $O_D(1)$, and
$\rho^\ast(q_0)\le2^{\omega(q_0)}\prod_{p\mid(q_0,2D)}(p+1)\ll_D2^{\omega(q_0)}$ the number of projective zeros modulo $q_0$; hence $\sum_{\text{levels}}\mathrm{diag}\ll_DH^{o(1)}uEM$.

\subsubsection*{Step S6: the level-summed off-diagonal}\label{sec:S6}
Let $a=u^2$. For $1\le Z_2\le H^{O(1)}$ we claim
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S6}
\sum_{q_0\le EM^2,\ (q_0,u)=1}\mathrm{off}(aq_0;Z_2)\ \ll_{f,\varepsilon}\ H^{\varepsilon}(1+Z_2),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $u$. Fix a pair $(w_1,z_2)$ of distinct family points with invariant $N=\langle w_1,z_2\rangle/u^2\in(2h,M]$, $M=2h(1+2Z_2^2)$. A common projective zero of
$Q_{w_1},Q_{z_2}$ modulo $p^k$ forces $p^k\mid\mathrm{Res}=\langle w_1,z_2\rangle^2-4\det w_1\det z_2=u^4(N^2-4h^2)$, because $x^3\mathrm{Res},y^3\mathrm{Res}\in(Q_{w_1},Q_{z_2})$, and Hensel's
lemma gives at most $2$ zeros of each form modulo $p^k$ when $p\nmid2ah$. The $O_D(1)$ primes $p\mid2D$ are pulled out first with the trivial factor $p+1$ each, at most once
since $v_p(q_0)\le1$ there ($j$ is squarefree and $(em,2D)=1$); the primes of $u$ do not divide $q_0$, the family $\mathcal S_{u^2,h}(q_0)$ being empty otherwise
($Aq_0C'-u^2B'^2=h$ would force $p\mid h$); so the $u^4$ is inert, and, the number of $(j,e,m)$ with a given $\mathrm{lcm}$ being at most $\tau(q_0)^2=H^{o(1)}$,
\[
\sum_{(j,e,m):\,e\le E,\,m\le M}n_{\mathrm{lcm}(j,e,m^2)}(w_1,z_2)\ \le\ \prod_{p\mid2D}(p+1)\cdot H^{o(1)}\sum_{q_0'\mid N^2-4h^2,\ (q_0',2Dah)=1}2^{\omega(q_0')}\ \ll_D\ H^{o(1)}\tau_3(N^2-4h^2),
\]
using $\sum_{d\mid n}2^{\omega(d)}\le\tau_3(n)$. The level sum therefore costs a divisor factor, not a factor $EM^2$. Now $N^2-4h^2\ge4h+1>0$ for distinct points, $\tau_3(N^2-4h^2)\ll M^{\varepsilon}$,
$M\asymp hZ_2^2\le H^{O(1)}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:Aoff} (steps (2)--(4), with $P_1(N)\ll(hN)^{o(1)}h^{1/2}$ and $\sum_{2h<N\le M}N^{-1/2}g_2(N)\le3\tau_3(u)M^{1/2}$) give
$\sum_{q_0}\mathrm{off}(aq_0;Z_2)\ll H^{\varepsilon}2^{\omega(u)}\tau_3(u)h^{3/2}M^{1/2}\ll H^{\varepsilon}(1+Z_2)$, the $u$-factor $6^{\omega(u)}$ being pointwise a divisor-size factor
and averaged in the application by $\sum_{u\sim U}6^{\omega(u)}/u\asymp(\log U)^5$. This proves~\eqref{eq:S6}. (The hypothesis $Z_2\le H^{O(1)}$ is where the $\varepsilon$-powers,
all of which are $M^{\varepsilon}$, become $H^{\varepsilon}$; it holds in Step~S7 after the dichotomy there.) Together with Step~S5, $\sum_{\text{levels}}\mathcal K_2\ll H^{o(1)}(uEM+Z_2)$.

\subsubsection*{Step S7: the per-box bound}
Fix a box $(L,K)$ and a block $(E,M)$ of levels, and apply~\eqref{eq:GM} to every level with the same $Z_0,Z_1,Z_2$. Pull the coefficients out by $|\kappa(e)\mu(m)|\ll H^{o(1)}/E$
--- $\mu$ has no decay in $m$, and this costs a factor $M$ --- and apply Cauchy--Schwarz over the $EM$ levels:
\[
\sum_{\text{levels}}|\kappa(e)\mu(m)|\,|\mathcal A_1|\ \ll\ H^{o(1)}Y^{C\eta'}\frac YK\,\frac1E\,(Lu)^{1/2}Z_0^{\theta}\Bigl(\sum\mathcal K_1\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum\mathcal K_2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
If $uEM\le L^{1/2}$ choose $Z_1=u^2EM$, $Z_2=uEM$ and $Z_0=Lu/(Z_1Z_2)=L/(u^2E^2M^2)\ge1$; then by~\eqref{eq:K1} and Step~S6 the right-hand side is
$\ll H^{o(1)}Y^{C\eta'+3\delta+\eta}\,\frac YK\,\frac{L}{EM}\cdot M\Bigl(\frac{u^2E^2M^2}L\Bigr)^{1/2-\theta}$: the block's own trivial size $(Y/K)L/(EM)$ times $M(u^2E^2M^2/L)^{1/2-\theta}$.
If $uEM>L^{1/2}$ the block is bounded by its trivial size $(Y/K)L/(EM)<(Y/K)L^{1/2}u$, and these blocks sum geometrically. Since the block bound decays like $E^{-2\theta}$ against a
trivial size like $1/E$, all dyadic $E$ sum without truncation, and $M\le Y^{\eta}$ gives $\sum_MM^{1-2\theta}\ll Y^{\eta(1-2\theta)}$. Hence, for the box,
\[
\sum_{\text{blocks}}\ \ll\ H^{o(1)}Y^{C\eta'+3\delta+\eta}\,\frac YK\Bigl(L^{1/2+\theta}u^{1-2\theta}Y^{\eta(1-2\theta)}+L^{1/2}u\Bigr)
=\ \frac YK\,L\cdot H^{o(1)}Y^{O(\delta+\eta+\eta')}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{u^2}L\Bigr)^{(1-2\theta)/2}+\frac u{L^{1/2}}\Bigr],
\]
a relative saving $(u^2/L)^{(1-2\theta)/2}Y^{O(\delta+\eta+\eta')}$ against the box's trivial size, with $u^2/L\le u^2/Y^{1-3\delta}$.

\subsubsection*{Step S8: parameters and summation}
Summing over the $O(\log^2H)$ boxes and adding the errors of~\eqref{eq:S1out}, each piece satisfies
\[
\mathcal P_u(H/u)\ \ll\ \frac Hu\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{u^3}H\Bigr)^{(1-2\theta)/2}H^{C\eta'+2\eta+O(\delta)}+H^{-\min(\delta,\eta,2\eta'-\delta)+\varepsilon}\Bigr],
\]
using $u^2/Y=u^3/H$. With $\theta=7/64$, $\frac32(1-2\theta)=1.171875$, and for $u\le H^{1/3-c}$ the first bracket is $\le H^{-1.171875c+C\eta'+2\eta+O(\delta)}$. Take $C=6$
(Remark~\ref{rem:CGM}) and $\delta=\eta=\eta'=t$ with $9t=1.171875c$, so that $C\eta'+2\eta+O(\delta)\le8t$ and the first term is $\le H^{-t}$, while $\min(\delta,\eta,2\eta'-\delta)=t$.
Finally $\sum_{u\le H^{1/3-c}}|w(u)|(H/u)^{1-t}\ll H^{1-t}\sum_{u\le H^{1/3}}|w(u)|u^{t-1}\ll H^{1-t+t/3}(\log H)^{O(1)}=H^{1-2t/3+o(1)}$, since $\sum_{u\le U}|w(u)|\ll U$. This is
Theorem~\ref{thm:S} with $\delta_S(c)=2t/3=\frac23\cdot\frac{1.171875c}9\approx0.087c$. \qed

\section{What remains: the band at $u=H^{1/3}$}
Theorems~\ref{thm:M} and~\ref{thm:S} leave a neighbourhood of $\alpha=1/3$ uncovered, and the two mechanisms fail there for unrelated reasons. On the upper side the balanced
Type~II atoms sit at $d^{1/4}$, where the available energy input for reciprocals is exactly at its threshold: what would be needed is a bound for the six-fold energy
$J_6(N)$ below $N^4$ at $N\asymp m^{1/4}$, which is not known even for prime moduli. On the lower side the kernel method's diagonal, the number of orbits of the family, is
$\asymp u$, and at $u\asymp Y^{1/2}$ it meets the trivial bound.

Three remarks fix what such a band costs, and what closing it would buy.

\begin{remark}[a band is cheap, and a failing method is free]
Inside any range of $\alpha$ one still has part~III's Theorem~E piece by piece, $\mathcal P_u(H/u)\ll(H/u)(\log H)^{-c}\log\log H$ uniformly in $u\le H^{1-\varepsilon}$. Hence a band
$[H^{\alpha_0-\delta},H^{\alpha_0+\delta}]$ on which nothing better is available costs $\ll_f\delta\cdot H(\log H)^{1-c}\log\log H$, not $\delta H\log H$: a mechanism that
degenerates can never make the total worse than what is already proved, and any band whose width tends to $0$ improves Theorem~E by that factor.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[a point gap costs $O(H)$]
Suppose the savings on the two sides vanish only at $\alpha=1/3$, say like $H^{-c|\alpha-1/3|}$, with implied constants that are powers of $\log H$. Summing over dyadic blocks,
$\sum_kH\,\mathrm{e}^{-c|k\log2-\frac13\log H|}=H\bigl(1+2/(\mathrm{e}^{c\log2}-1)\bigr)=O(H/c)$: the blocks near the point contribute $O(H)$ in total. Since the main term
$c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H$ comes from $u>H^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ and the remaining blocks are error terms, this would give $\Off^\ast_f(H)=O(H)$, which is Hypothesis~(E) of
part~I~\cite{ReichardtI} and yields $\sum_{h\le H}(1-h/H)(S_f(h)-C(f)^2)=-\tfrac12C(f)\log H+O_f(1)$: a bounded second-order term.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what $A_f$ needs]
The constant of Conjecture~1 of part~I requires $\Off^\ast_f(H)=c_{\mathrm{off}}(f)H+o(H)$, hence a saving factor $\gg\log H$ uniformly on $|\alpha-1/3|\ll\log\log H/\log H$; a
fixed positive power saving at $\alpha=1/3$ itself suffices. A linearly vanishing saving, however small the rate, gives $O(H)$ but never $o(H)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what is actually binding]
An audit of every implied constant in the chain (research/explore/PROOFS-uniform.md §36) shows that no divisor-size loss constrains the argument: the divisor factors all occur
under sums and average to powers of $\log H$, the kernel theorem's $\delta^{-O(1)}$ is $\delta^{-20}$, its $(AD)^{o(1)}$ is $(\log AD)^{O(1)}$, and its $q^{o(1)}$ is $1$ in the
present application. What binds is the constant in Bourgain and Garaev's H\"older step, which grows as $k_1\asymp 2/(9|\alpha-1/3|)\to\infty$: the band on which the estimate is
effective has width $\asymp(\log\log H/\log H)^{1/3}$. With the in-band fallback on part~III's Theorem~E this gives, once the map is written out,
$\Off^\ast_f(H)\ll H(\log H)^{0.63+o(1)}$ for $D<0$, while everything outside the band sums to $O(H)$. The step from there to $\Off^\ast_f(H)=O(H)$ therefore needs one thing only:
an estimate that survives at the exceptional sizes $N\asymp m^{1/4}$ --- an energy bound $J_6$ below $N^4$ for composite moduli, or a different tool there. A level-averaged
spectral bound for the twist $\mathrm{e}(cr\bar d/x)$ with $x\asymp Y^{1/2}$ would serve the same purpose; neither is in the literature.
\end{remark}
